What If AI Factored the Weather Forecast Into Your Itinerary? Easing Rainy-Day Worries

2026-07-29 | myTravel Team

No matter how flawless your travel plan looks on paper, it means little if the sky refuses to cooperate. When rain pours down on the day you booked an open-air observation deck, or a heat wave rolls in on the day you scheduled an all-day walking tour, you end up with a schedule your body simply can't keep up with. That's why seasoned travelers always ask themselves "what will the weather be like that day?" as they plan.

Lately, AI travel tools take on part of that mental load. myTravel's AI itinerary feature works by consulting the weather forecast for your destination and suggesting indoor-focused activities on days when rain is likely. In this article, we lay out honestly how AI actually factors in the weather, where its limits lie, and how you can refine your itinerary yourself to stand up to the weather.

How AI 'Reads' the Weather and Works It Into Your Itinerary

Saying AI factors in the weather can sound like magic, but the mechanism is simpler than you'd think. When myTravel's AI itinerary feature receives your destination and dates, it also pulls in weather forecast data from OpenWeather. It then uses that forecast as reference material when generating the itinerary.

The key is adjusting "what's a good outdoor thing to do on which day" to match the forecast. On days expected to be clear, it's easy to slot in outdoor activities like observation decks, parks, and walking tours; on days when rain is expected, it prioritizes activities you can enjoy indoors. It's essentially the AI automatically doing what a person does when they check the forecast and think, "I should bring an umbrella that day — let's plan an indoor route."

Good to know What the AI consults is, ultimately, a 'forecast.' Forecasts can differ from the actual weather, so it's best to treat the AI-generated itinerary as one reference plan and adjust it to conditions on the ground.

Forecasts Reach Only Five Days Out — Long Trips See Just the First Stretch Covered

There's a limit we should point out honestly right up front. The OpenWeather forecast that myTravel pulls in covers up to five days. That's as far as the freely available forecast range goes.

In other words, for a trip longer than five days, only the early portion has the weather factored in, while the later portion does not. For example, if you generate a 10-day itinerary with AI, the first few days are adjusted between indoor and outdoor based on the forecast, but from day six onward the schedule is built on general assumptions without weather information.

So here's how to make good use of it

  • Short trips (2–5 days): This is the range where you get the fullest benefit of weather-based planning. It's easy to take the AI draft's indoor/outdoor layout more or less as is.
  • Long trips (6 days or more): Trust the AI's suggestions for the early stretch, but for the later part it's realistic to check the forecast yourself and make adjustments as departure draws near.
  • Very long trips: myTravel can generate trips of up to 31 days, but no forecast can tell you precisely what the weather will be a month out. Approach the later dates with a sense of the 'seasonal average.'

To sum up, weather integration works most powerfully for 'the first few days as departure approaches.' Once you understand this characteristic, you can use AI itineraries far more wisely.

When Rain Is Likely, It Suggests Indoor Alternatives

This is the most practical part of weather integration. When the chance of rain on a given day is 50% or higher, myTravel's AI prioritizes alternative activities you can enjoy indoors that day.

Specifically, places like museums, art galleries, and cafés — spots that let you stay out of the rain while keeping the joy of travel going — become candidates. Instead of a route that has you walking outside for hours, it sets the tone of the day with an indoor route where the flow won't be interrupted even if it rains.

Destinations where indoor alternatives really shine

  • Cities rich in galleries and museums: In places with plenty of indoor attractions like Paris, London, and New York, a rainy day can actually turn into a day of cultural exploration.
  • Regions with a rainy season: In places prone to sudden downpours, like Southeast Asia, lining up indoor alternatives in advance can greatly affect how satisfying the trip feels.
  • Cities with a strong café culture: Two hours by a window seat in a café on a rainy day becomes an unforgettable scene of the trip in its own right.
Practical tip Even if the AI doesn't suggest indoor alternatives, it's reassuring to list two or three 'indoor places to go when it rains' for each destination yourself. That way you won't be caught off guard if the forecast misses and rain suddenly arrives.

AI Generation Takes Time — Not 'Instant,' but 'a Short Wait'

Here's another point we'll be honest about. Building an itinerary that even factors in the weather takes the AI roughly 10 to 90 seconds. The longer the trip and the more information there is to factor in, the longer it takes.

Rather than results popping up the moment you press the button, it goes through a computation process that combines your destination, dates, and the forecast to assemble each day's activities. Think of that brief wait as a sign that multiple conditions are being worked into your schedule.

There are also policies around how many times you can generate. myTravel's AI itinerary generation is available 3 times a month for free members and 30 times a month for premium members. So rather than endlessly re-rolling until you're satisfied, it's far more efficient to refine one draft by hand into a finished plan.

This is a misconception The expectation that AI will churn out a flawless itinerary in the blink of an eye with no limit on attempts doesn't match reality. In practice it takes anywhere from 10 to 90 seconds, and the number of generations is capped. Use each generation with care, and focus on refining the result you get.

What AI Doesn't Do — No Real-Time Alerts or Automatic Rescheduling

To use the weather-integration feature properly, you also need to know exactly what AI doesn't do. Clearing away inflated expectations keeps you from being disappointed and makes it clear where you'll need to fill in yourself.

The weather that myTravel's AI factors in is the forecast at the moment the itinerary is generated. In other words, once you've set off and the weather changes on the ground, the AI won't rebuild your itinerary on its own. There's also no feature that sends real-time alerts during your trip like "it's raining now, switch to indoors."

So these are up to you

  • Rechecking the forecast just before the trip: If the forecast has shifted significantly a few days before departure, regenerate the itinerary or edit it yourself to reflect the change.
  • Manual adjustment on the ground: If the weather suddenly turns bad, just open that day's schedule and swap in indoor activities. myTravel itineraries can be freely edited even during the trip.
  • Preparing alternatives in advance: If you attach a note like "if it rains, go here" next to an outdoor activity, you can respond right away when the situation changes.

To sum up, AI plays the role of producing 'a solid draft that reflects the forecast at the moment of generation,' while keeping the itinerary alive and responsive to the changing weather during the trip still comes down to the traveler's own judgment.

Practical Tips for a Weather-Resilient Itinerary

Whether you get help from AI or plan it yourself, itineraries that don't get thrown off by the weather share some common traits. Learn the principles below and your trip won't fall apart even when the forecast misses.

1. Mix outdoor and indoor within a single day

If you fill an entire day with nothing but outdoor activities, you have no fallback when it rains that day. Mix two kinds of activities within a day — outdoor in the morning, indoor in the afternoon — and you won't have to tear up the whole schedule no matter which way the weather turns.

2. Place weather-dependent activities on 'flexible days'

For activities that are only worthwhile in clear weather — observation decks, hiking, beaches — leave room to shift them to a day with a good forecast rather than pinning them to a single day of the trip. On a multi-day trip, it's better to flexibly assign 'the observation deck on the clearest day.'

3. Secure a backup indoor route in advance

If you scout out two or three indoor attractions at your destination in advance (galleries, museums, large shopping malls, aquariums, and the like), you'll have cards ready to pull out immediately on a rainy day.

Add a sense of the seasons, too For distant dates the forecast can't reach, approach them with 'the average for that region in that season.' Knowing seasonal traits — Southeast Asia's rainy season, the short daylight of a European winter, Japan's rainy season — lets you prepare even without a forecast.

When AI Weather Integration Is Most Useful

As with any feature, AI weather integration has situations it suits best. Knowing which trips it especially shines in raises how well you can put it to use.

  • Short trips with a near departure: Since a forecast within five days is close to reality, you can trust the AI's indoor/outdoor layout almost as is.
  • Destinations with fickle weather: In places where rain is frequent, like mountain regions or areas with a rainy season, indoor-alternative suggestions serve as real insurance.
  • Trips where you can't spend much time planning: Building a plan from scratch while also accounting for the weather takes a lot of effort. Sketching the framework with an AI draft and refining only the details can save a great deal of time.

Conversely, for a long trip in the distant future where the forecast isn't out yet, the benefit of weather integration is limited. In that case, it's better to use AI as 'a tool for shaping your route and choosing attractions' and cover weather preparedness with seasonal knowledge and on-the-ground checks.

Wrapping Up: AI Reads the Forecast, but You Make the Call

Weather is the hardest variable to control in travel. That's exactly why a feature that 'plans your itinerary with the weather in mind' is a reassuring ally. myTravel's AI consults your destination's forecast up to five days out and, on days when rain is expected, suggests indoor alternatives to lend a hand with the traveler's plans.

Its limits are just as clear, though. Forecasts reach only five days, so long trips see only the early portion covered; generation takes 10 to 90 seconds; and you use it within the range of 3 times a month for free and 30 times a month for premium. Above all, it won't detect the weather in real time during your trip and reschedule automatically, so adjusting the itinerary to changing conditions still comes down to the traveler.

In the end, the best approach is a collaboration between the two. Once the AI reads the forecast and produces a solid draft, you layer backup indoor routes on top, place outdoor activities flexibly, and make the final call on the ground while looking up at the sky. If you're preparing your next trip, enter your destination and dates in myTravel to get a forecast-informed draft, then refine it with the principles we've laid out today. That's where a trip that won't be shaken by the rain begins.
