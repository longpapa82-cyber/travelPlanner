New York 4-Day First-Trip Itinerary: A Clear Route Through Midtown, Downtown, and Central Park

2026-07-30 | Hoonjae Park · myTravel Travel Content Team

Your First Time in New York: Why 4 Days Is Just Right

New York packs its attractions so densely that even a short trip lets you feel the full intensity of the city. But once you actually sit down to plan, the list of places you want to see never seems to end—Times Square, the Statue of Liberty, Central Park, the Brooklyn Bridge, the museums—and it can feel overwhelming.

Four days is a comfortable amount of time to cover the core of Manhattan without rushing. Dedicate one day to Midtown (Times Square, observation decks, Fifth Avenue), one day to Downtown (the Statue of Liberty, Wall Street, the 9/11 Memorial, SoHo), and one day to Central Park, the museums, and the Brooklyn Bridge. Grouping the areas this way keeps them from getting mixed up and cuts down on wasted travel. Manhattan is a long, narrow island running north to south, so the key is to divide it into upper and lower sections as you go.

This article is organized around day-by-day routes grouped by area, so a first-time visitor to New York can follow it as is.

Check before you leave The United States is not visa-free for many travelers—you need to apply online for an ESTA (Electronic System for Travel Authorization) and receive approval before departure. Approval can take some time, so once you've booked your flight, apply as early as you can. It's also a good idea to check your passport's expiration date at the same time.

Day 1: Arrival and Midtown

It depends on your arrival time, but assuming you land in the afternoon, a good first day runs through Midtown in the order hotel check-in → Times Square → observation deck → Fifth Avenue.

From the Airport to the City

  • JFK Airport: Take the AirTrain and transfer to the subway to reach Manhattan. If you have a lot of luggage or arrive at night, a yellow cab or a ride-hail is more convenient, and there are segments where a flat fare applies to Manhattan.
  • Newark Airport: The AirTrain connects to New Jersey Transit trains, which take you to Penn Station.
  • LaGuardia Airport: It's the closest to Manhattan, but there's no direct subway link, so you'll use a bus plus subway or a taxi.

Times Square

The scene created by wall-to-wall electronic billboards and huge crowds is a symbol of New York. The Broadway theater district is clustered nearby, making it a great spot to work an evening musical into your plan.

Observation Decks and Fifth Avenue

The Empire State Building and Rockefeller Center's Top of the Rock are the signature observation decks. Both charge admission, and sunset is more popular than daytime, so it helps to set a time in advance. Walk along Fifth Avenue from Rockefeller Center and you'll pass a string of department stores and flagship brand shops.

Day 2: Downtown and SoHo

The second day is built around Downtown in the south of Manhattan. The Statue of Liberty, Wall Street, the 9/11 Memorial, and SoHo are close enough together to link on foot, so you can cover a full, satisfying day.

Morning: The Statue of Liberty

The ferry to Liberty Island is popular, so booking in advance is recommended. If time is tight or ferry tickets are hard to get, the Staten Island Ferry is free and passes right by Liberty Island, letting you see the Statue of Liberty from the boat. If you just want a photo, this free ferry is a great-value alternative.

Afternoon: Wall Street and the 9/11 Memorial

Pass through Wall Street, the heart of finance, and the Charging Bull, then take in the two enormous reflecting pools of the 9/11 Memorial built on the site of the former World Trade Center. The One World Observatory charges separate admission.

Evening: SoHo

SoHo, with its cast-iron buildings and cobblestone streets, is full of concept stores, stylish cafes, and restaurants—perfect for combining dinner and shopping. You can keep walking on to nearby Little Italy and Chinatown as well.

  • Getting-around tip: Battery Park (the ferry terminal) → Wall Street → the 9/11 Memorial → SoHo flows naturally over one or two subway stops or on foot.

Day 3: Central Park, the Metropolitan, and the Brooklyn Bridge

Fill the morning of the third day with Central Park and the Metropolitan Museum of Art, then in the afternoon walk the Brooklyn Bridge and take in New York's skyline.

Morning: Central Park

This enormous park in the middle of the city is free to enter. Take a slow walk past Bethesda Fountain, Strawberry Fields, and the lake to step away from the city's pace for a while. The mood changes completely with the seasons.

Midday: The Metropolitan Museum of Art

Right along the eastern edge of Central Park, the Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) boasts a world-class collection. A single day isn't enough to see it all, so it's best to decide in advance which galleries you're most interested in.

Afternoon: The Brooklyn Bridge

Walk across the bridge from Manhattan toward Brooklyn and Manhattan's skyscrapers spread out across the river. After crossing, look for the photo spot in the DUMBO neighborhood where the bridge and buildings overlap. It's especially beautiful around sunset.

Day 4: Morning Shopping and Departure

On the last day, wrap up with a morning plan followed by the trip to the airport, timed to your flight. Avoid hauling heavy luggage far, and plan your final stops close to your hotel or the airport route.

Recommended Wrap-Up Spots

  • Fifth Avenue and Herald Square: Large department stores and brand shops are clustered here, making it easy to buy gifts and souvenirs all in one place.
  • Grand Central Terminal: Take in the grand interior of the historic station, and grab a light meal in the lower-level dining concourse.
  • The High Line and Chelsea Market: A garden walkway built on a former rail line, plus an indoor market—great for a light morning stroll.

When to Arrive at the Airport

For international flights, arriving at the airport 2 to 3 hours before departure is the safe choice. Checking bags and going through security takes longer than you might expect, so give yourself some buffer. JFK and Newark are far from the city and prone to traffic jams, so it's safest to avoid rush hour and allow plenty of travel time.

New York Transit, Tipping Culture, and Budget Guide

What first-time visitors to New York wonder about most is the subway and tipping culture. The subway may look like it has a lot of lines, but if you just enter your start and end points in the Google Maps app, it will guide you through transfers and travel time—so there's no need to worry much.

Transit

  • Subway (24-hour service): This is the backbone of getting around Manhattan. The network is dense, so it reaches just about any tourist spot.
  • OMNY and MetroCard: At the turnstile, OMNY lets you tap a contactless card or your smartphone directly, while the MetroCard is a rechargeable card. If you plan to ride many times, taking advantage of the fare cap (no extra charge beyond a certain number of rides) works in your favor.

Tipping Culture

Tipping is effectively part of the culture in the United States. At restaurants you typically leave 15–20% of the pre-tax amount as a tip, and you add a similar level for taxis and ride-hails. When paying by card, the screen often shows tip-percentage options to choose from. Factor tipping into your budget from the start.

Rough 4-Day Budget (per person)

  • Airfare: Roughly USD 700–1,300 depending on the season
  • Accommodation: About USD 320–850 for 3 nights (prices are high, so the range is wide)
  • Food: USD 60–120 per day (including tips)
  • Transit: USD 7–15 per day
  • Admissions and shopping: Observation decks, museums, ferries, and so on vary a lot by person, but allow around USD 150–280 to be safe

The currency is the US dollar (USD), and how expensive things feel changes a lot with the exchange rate. It's a good idea to check the rate before you leave and set your budget accordingly.
