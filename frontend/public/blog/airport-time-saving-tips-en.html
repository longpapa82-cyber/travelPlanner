7 Ways to Save Time at the Airport

2026-02-08 | myTravel Team

The airport is where a trip begins, and it is also one of the most stressful places along the way. Long check-in lines, complicated security screening, crowded duty-free shops, the long walk to the gate — the time wasted at an airport adds up more than you might expect. At large international airports in particular, it is not unusual for two or three hours to slip by between check-in and boarding. If you can cut that time down efficiently, your trip will feel far more relaxed and enjoyable right from the start.

Most wasted airport time comes down to a lack of preparation. A little planning ahead can dramatically shorten the time you spend in line and free you up to relax in a lounge or enjoy some duty-free shopping instead. In this article, we'll walk through seven key ways to save time at the airport, along with special tips for major airports around the world and how to handle flight delays — everything you need to know to move through the airport with ease.

1. Use Online Check-In and Mobile Boarding Passes

One of the biggest time sinks at any airport is the check-in counter. During peak seasons or on weekends, check-in lines can stretch dozens of meters, and waiting 30 minutes to over an hour is not uncommon. The way to eliminate this time entirely is online check-in and a mobile boarding pass.

Most airlines offer online check-in starting 24 to 48 hours before departure through their website or app. When you check in online, you can also select your seat in advance and receive a mobile boarding pass. With a mobile boarding pass in hand, there's no need to get a paper pass at the airport, so if you have no checked baggage, you can skip the check-in counter entirely. The QR code on your phone screen is all you need to clear security and pass through the boarding gate.

Even if you do have checked baggage, online check-in still pays off. Instead of the regular check-in line, you can use a dedicated bag drop counter, which is far shorter than the standard check-in line. At Incheon Airport, for example, you can print a baggage tag at a self check-in kiosk and place your bag on the Self Bag Drop station to complete baggage drop-off in one or two minutes. The whole process takes less than five minutes.

Online check-in tip: If you turn on notifications in your airline's app, you'll be alerted the moment check-in opens. Checking in right when it opens improves your chances of grabbing the seat you want, such as a window or aisle. Korean Air opens check-in 48 hours out, Asiana 48 hours out, and low-cost carriers usually open it 24 hours before departure.

2. Clear Security Screening Quickly

Security screening is the second most time-consuming stage at the airport. But with good preparation, you can significantly shorten your time getting through. The key is to remove anything that might get flagged at security ahead of time.

First, prepare your liquids according to the rules. Carry-on liquids must be no more than 100ml per container with a total of no more than 1 liter, and they must be placed in a clear zip-top bag (no larger than 20cm x 20cm). Keep this bag somewhere easy to pull out of your carry-on. Taking the bag out and placing it in a separate tray lets the X-ray inspection move along quickly. If you're not prepared, you'll waste time digging through your bag for your liquids and get impatient looks from the people behind you in line.

Second, organize your electronics so they're easy to grab. Larger electronics like laptops and tablets have to be taken out of your bag and placed in a separate tray. If you keep your laptop in the top compartment or a dedicated pocket of your bag, you can pull it out fast. Some airports, including Incheon, have introduced high-performance CT scanners that let you leave your laptop in your bag, but they aren't in place at every lane yet, so it's safest to be ready to take it out.

Third, remove metal items in advance. Belts, watches, coins, keys, and other metal objects set off the metal detector. Putting them in your coat pocket or bag before you reach the checkpoint means you won't get flagged during the body scan, which saves time. Shoes with lots of metal detailing or thick boots sometimes have to come off too, so it's a good idea to wear comfortable slip-on shoes at the airport.

Fourth, take advantage of Fast Track service. At some airports, you can use a dedicated security lane for an extra fee. It's also often offered free of charge to business-class and above passengers or to elite-tier airline members. At Incheon Airport, the departure Fast Track is available to business-class and above passengers or airline lounge members, and it can cut waiting time by 70 to 80 percent compared with regular security.

3. Make the Most of Airport Lounges

Airport lounges aren't just for business-class passengers. There are several ways for regular travelers to access lounges too, and using one can turn your airport waiting time into time for rest and recharging.

Getting in through a premium credit card is the most common route. Many premium cards with an annual fee include free access to domestic and international airport lounges. If you hold a card that comes with a Priority Pass, you can use more than 1,500 airport lounges worldwide. The number of visits per year varies by card, but for frequent travelers it can easily be worth more than the annual fee.

You can also buy a day pass. At Incheon Airport, for example, you can purchase a day pass on-site or online at lounges such as Matina Lounge and SKYHUB Lounge. Prices are usually around 30,000 to 50,000 won and include food, drinks, Wi-Fi, shower facilities, and comfortable seating. It's especially useful when a long wait is expected or when you're flying early in the morning or late at night.

When choosing a lounge, picking one near your departure gate helps save time. At Incheon Airport Terminal 2, for instance, you have to take a shuttle train to the concourse, so using a lounge in the concourse cuts down on travel time. Most lounges have a flight information monitor so you can check gate changes or delays in real time.

Lounge tip: When using a lounge, head to your gate at least 30 minutes before boarding. It's easy to lose track of time while relaxing and occasionally miss boarding. Some lounges will page your flight if you let the staff know which one you're on.

4. Cut Shopping Time with Duty-Free Pre-Ordering

Duty-free shopping is one of the pleasures of traveling abroad, but wandering the shops to pick out products and pay for them takes a good deal of time. During peak seasons, popular brand shops can have long lines. Using duty-free pre-ordering (online duty-free) can cut this time way down.

Major duty-free retailers such as Shilla, Lotte, and Shinsegae all offer online ordering. Order and pay for the products you want online before departure, and all you have to do is pick them up at the duty-free pickup counter inside the departure area. No wandering the shops, no standing in line. Online duty-free often has bigger discounts than the physical stores, and you can also earn various coupons and reward points, so it's better on price too.

Pickup counters are usually located near the boarding gates, so the walking route is efficient. At Incheon Airport Terminal 1, there are pickup counters on the east side (gates 26 to 50) and the west side (gates 1 to 25), and Terminal 2 also has pickup counters inside the departure area. When you pre-order, choosing a pickup point close to your boarding gate minimizes travel time.

That said, pickup counters can have lines too, so it's safest to collect your items at least one hour before boarding. Some duty-free retailers also offer a service to reserve a pickup time slot, which can shorten your wait even further.

5. Book Airport Transportation in Advance

You can save time getting to the airport as well. If you only look into transportation on the morning of departure, you might hit peak-hour traffic and take far longer than expected, or scramble because you can't find a taxi. Booking transportation in advance removes these variables.

The airport limousine bus is the most economical choice. Incheon Airport limousines depart from major points in Seoul, and fares run around 10,000 to 17,000 won. The seats are spacious and there's plenty of room for luggage, so it's a comfortable ride. Travel time can vary with traffic, though, so aim to arrive with a comfortable margin, 2.5 to 3 hours ahead. You can check limousine bus schedules and stop locations on the Incheon Airport website or app.

The Airport Railroad (AREX) is the most punctual option. The express train runs from Seoul Station to Incheon Airport in 43 minutes, and the all-stop train takes about 66 minutes. The express train allows seat reservations, and at Seoul Station you can complete city airport terminal check-in and baggage drop-off all in one place. Using this service lets you skip check-in and baggage drop at the airport, saving a great deal of time. Korean Air, Asiana, and Jeju Air all offer the Seoul Station city airport terminal service.

Taxis and ride-hailing services are the most convenient. Booking in advance through apps like Kakao Taxi or Tada gets a car to you right at your departure time. It's especially handy for early-morning or dawn flights. It's the most expensive option, though, and during rush hour, traffic can make it take longer than expected. With two or more people you can split the fare, which makes it more economical.

6. How to Use Your Layover Time

When you travel on a connecting flight, transit time can easily slip away in boredom. But used well, a layover can actually become a bonus part of your trip. Here are some tips for making the most of your transit time.

First, map out your transit route in advance. Connecting at a large airport often requires changing terminals, and moving between terminals alone can take 20 to 30 minutes. Before you leave, check the terminal map of your connecting airport and figure out the route from your arrival gate to your departure gate ahead of time. Airport apps and websites provide terminal maps and transit guides for reference.

If your layover is four hours or more, you may even be able to leave the airport for a bit of sightseeing. Incheon Airport, for example, runs a free transit tour program with 1-hour to 5-hour courses touring Incheon or Seoul. Singapore's Changi Airport also offers a free city tour, and Istanbul Airport provides a free Istanbul tour for Turkish Airlines transit passengers. When leaving the airport, be sure to check the visa requirements and leave plenty of time to get back in.

If your layover is two to three hours, it's better to make use of airport facilities. Most large international airports have free Wi-Fi, charging stations, rest areas, kids' zones, and more. Incheon Airport has free cultural experience programs (hanbok fittings, traditional crafts, and more), free shower facilities, massage chairs, and even a movie theater, so your transit time won't feel dull. If you have lounge access, take the chance to shower and eat during your layover and recharge for the next flight.

Recommended layover times: For a same-airline, same-terminal connection, allow at least 1 hour 30 minutes; if you need to change terminals, at least 2 hours 30 minutes; and if you need to travel to a different airport, at least 4 hours. Moving between Incheon Airport Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 takes about 15 minutes by shuttle train.

7. Moving Quickly After Arrival: From Immigration to Airport Exit

There are ways to save time after you land at your destination airport too. Handling immigration, baggage claim, customs, and the trip from the airport into the city efficiently means you can make the most of your very first day.

Use automated immigration (SES). Korean passport holders can use automated immigration not only at Incheon Airport but in many countries, including Japan (Narita, Kansai, and others), Australia, and Singapore. Scan your passport and go through facial or fingerprint recognition, and immigration is done in seconds. Whereas the regular immigration line can take 30 minutes to an hour, automated screening is over in one or two minutes. Check each country's immigration rules in advance to see whether automated screening is available.

You can also use priority baggage handling. Business-class and above passengers or elite-tier airline members have a Priority tag on their bags so they come out first. Even economy passengers can get priority baggage service by joining an airline's frequent-flyer program and building up miles. Alternatively, if you travel with carry-on only and no checked baggage, you can skip the baggage wait entirely. For a short 3-to-4-day trip, a single carry-on can be plenty.

Book transportation from the airport to the city in advance too. Looking for transportation on-site after you arrive wastes time. Research airport pickup services, airport limousines, airport railways, and the like ahead of time and book them if you can. Buying a NEX (Narita Express) ticket in advance for the trip from Tokyo's Narita Airport into the city is cheaper than buying on-site and saves time as well. If you install overseas taxi apps like Grab and Bolt in advance and register a payment method, you can hail a car the moment you arrive.

Special Incheon Airport Tips: Getting Full Value from a World-Class Airport

Incheon Airport has topped global airport service rankings for years, and it lives up to that reputation with a wide range of facilities and services for passenger convenience. Knowing and using these services lets you spend your time at Incheon much more efficiently.

The Seoul Station City Airport Terminal is one of Incheon Airport's best services. You can complete check-in, baggage drop-off, and even departure immigration all at Seoul Station, so when you reach Incheon Airport you can go straight into the duty-free area. You must finish processing at Seoul Station by 3 hours before departure, and Korean Air, Asiana, Jeju Air, T'way Air, and others support the service. It saves you the trouble of hauling heavy luggage to the airport and greatly reduces your waiting time there.

The Smart Pass is a fast departure service using biometrics. Register your face and passport information in advance, and on your departure day you can go from check-in to boarding without your passport or boarding pass, using facial recognition alone. Security, departure immigration, and the boarding gate all clear quickly through facial recognition, so your overall time is greatly reduced.

The free transit tour program is another service unique to Incheon Airport. For passengers with long layovers, it offers a variety of free tours ranging from a 1-hour course (Incheon city sightseeing) to a 5-hour course (Seoul city sightseeing). You can also join free Korean cultural experience programs such as hanbok fittings and traditional crafts. Check the Incheon Airport official website for tour schedules and how to reserve.

Tips for Major Airports Around the World

Narita Airport, Japan (NRT)

Narita Airport is about 60 to 90 minutes from central Tokyo, so travel time tends to be long. Taking the NEX (Narita Express) gets you to Shibuya in about 75 minutes and Shinjuku in about 80 minutes. JR East sells a discounted ticket exclusively for foreign visitors, so buying it in advance is cheaper. Terminal 3 is for low-cost carriers only and requires a 15-minute walk or a shuttle bus from Terminal 2. Be sure to factor in that travel time when using Terminal 3. For immigration, registering on Visit Japan Web in advance can save you time.

Heathrow Airport, UK (LHR)

Heathrow Airport has five terminals, so confirming your terminal is essential. Free shuttle trains run between terminals, but they can take 15 to 20 minutes. Into central London, the Heathrow Express is the fastest, reaching Paddington Station in 15 minutes. It's expensive, though (about 25 pounds one way), so if you have some time to spare, the Underground (Piccadilly line) gets you there in about an hour for around 6 pounds. The UK tends to have strict immigration, so have your accommodation booking confirmation and return flight details ready in advance.

JFK Airport, USA (JFK)

JFK has six terminals, and you use the AirTrain to move between them. Getting to Manhattan takes about 60 to 90 minutes via the AirTrain plus the subway (LIRR or NYC Subway). US immigration is famous for taking a long time, so preparing Global Entry or the MPC (Mobile Passport Control) app in advance can greatly shorten your immigration time. Korean nationals can apply for Global Entry too, and once approved, you can clear immigration in one or two minutes at a dedicated kiosk.

How to Handle Flight Delays

No matter how well you prepare, flight delays are a variable you can't control. But handling delays wisely can minimize the impact and reduce your stress.

First, turn on your airline app's notifications. Most airline apps send real-time push alerts for flight status changes (delays, gate changes, cancellations, and so on). Airport announcements are easy to miss in the noise, but you can rely on app notifications. Flight-tracking apps like FlightAware and Flightradar24 are useful too.

Second, request compensation from the airline for delays of two hours or more. For long delays caused by the airline (maintenance, crew issues, and the like), you may be entitled to compensation such as meal vouchers, lounge passes, or hotel accommodation (for overnight delays). For flights departing from or arriving in the EU, you can claim up to 600 euros in monetary compensation under EU261 regulations. To receive compensation, be sure to keep your boarding pass and a delay confirmation.

Third, research alternative flights in advance. Knowing ahead of time whether other airlines fly the same route, or whether a nearby airport could serve as an alternative, lets you find options quickly in the event of a cancellation or long delay. When requesting a rebooking at the airline counter, presenting specific alternatives gets it handled faster.

Delay-ready essentials: a phone charger (power bank), a light snack, water, a book or tablet, and a change of clothes (in case your connecting baggage doesn't arrive). Keeping these items in your carry-on lets you wait out an unexpected delay in comfort.
