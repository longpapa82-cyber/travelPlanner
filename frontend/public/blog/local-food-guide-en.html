How to Enjoy Local Food Right: From Ordering to Hygiene

2026-07-25 | myTravel Team

Food Leaves the Longest-Lasting Memory of a Trip

The sights you see live on in photos, but the memory of a taste is etched into you. A single bowl in a country can shape your whole impression of the trip. Yet enjoying food in an unfamiliar place has its own knack: not falling for tourist restaurants, ordering well, and eating safely without an upset stomach.

This guide covers four practical areas: finding the real gems, ordering tips, enjoying street food, and hygiene and food-poisoning prevention.

Avoid Tourist Traps and Find the Real Spots

The restaurant right in front of a famous landmark, with a big photo menu and someone waving you in, is usually for tourists. It sells on location rather than taste — often pricey and unremarkable.

There are signals for the real gems. Places full of locals, with a short menu focused on a specific dish, a block or two off the tourist strip, are generally trustworthy. When reading map-app reviews, favor spots with lots of recent reviews written in the local language over raw star counts. Picking restaurants in advance and adding them to your itinerary cuts down on hungry, regretful "let's just go here" moments.

For finding experiences you can only have locally, see enjoying local experiences.

Order Like This and Fail Less

You can order even without reading the menu. The camera function of a translation app pointed at the menu gives you the gist, and pointing at a good-looking dish on the next table works beautifully too.

In an unfamiliar country, it's safest to start with the region's signature dishes. Searching "must-eat food in (city)" beforehand keeps you from freezing at the menu. If you're allergic to certain spices or ingredients, note those words in the local language and show them when ordering to avoid misunderstandings. Pre-translating spice levels or "without this ingredient" requests helps too.

Street Food: Enjoy It, but Safely

Street food is the cheapest way to experience a country's real flavor. To enjoy it, follow a few safety rules.

Pick stalls with fast turnover — a line means the ingredients are fresh and not sitting out. Food cooked hot to order is safer than something pre-made and cooled. Drink bottled or sealed beverages, and in areas where hygiene is a concern, be cautious with ice and washed raw vegetables, since they're often made with tap water.

Preventing and Handling Food Poisoning

However careful you are, your body may not adjust to unfamiliar food and water. The basics of prevention are hand hygiene and water. Wash your hands or use sanitizer before eating, and where hygiene is shaky, even brush your teeth with bottled water.

If you do get sick, the priority is preventing dehydration. Drink plenty of clean water and electrolytes (sports drinks or oral rehydration), rest, and avoid irritating foods for a day or two — most cases recover. Packing anti-diarrheal medicine and electrolyte powder is handy. If symptoms are severe or persist, see a local clinic; for handling such emergencies, see the travel emergency guide.

Wrap-Up: Deliciously, and Safely

The key to enjoying local food is balancing two things: the adventurousness to skip the traps and find real flavor, and the caution to check turnover, cooking state, and water. Know the signature dishes and spots in advance, keep to the hygiene rules, and you can fully experience a country's flavors without an upset stomach.
