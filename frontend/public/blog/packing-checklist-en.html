The Complete International Travel Packing Checklist

2026-02-10 | myTravel Team

One of the most stressful moments before an international trip is packing. "Do I need this too?" "Did I forget anything?" These worries keep nagging right up until departure. In fact, many travelers only realize they've left something important behind after they've already reached the airport. People who left their passport at home, forgot a charger, or blanked on their medications—these slip-ups are more common than you'd think.

With a systematic checklist, you can prevent these mistakes almost entirely. This article gives you a complete, category-by-category packing checklist for international travel. From travel documents to clothing, toiletries, electronics, and other essentials, this one checklist will help you pack perfectly without leaving anything behind. Go through it item by item two or three days before departure.

Travel Documents: The First Things to Check

Travel documents are the very first thing to verify when you pack. You can buy almost anything else once you arrive, but a passport or visa simply cannot be replaced. Check the items below a week before you leave.

Your passport should have at least six months of validity remaining—always confirm this. Many countries require at least six months of passport validity on entry, so if yours is close to expiring, renew it in advance. Prepare a separate copy of your passport and keep it apart from the original. For extra peace of mind, save a scan of your passport to your email or cloud storage in case of an emergency.

Visas: always check the visa requirements of your destination country. Some countries allow visa-free entry, but others require an electronic travel authorization (such as an ETA or ESTA) to enter. The United States requires an ESTA, Canada an eTA, and Australia an ETA, all applied for in advance. It's safest to complete these steps at least 72 hours before departure.

Don't forget your booking confirmations either. Print out or save to your phone your flight confirmation, hotel reservation, rental car booking, and so on. Some countries ask for proof of accommodation at immigration. Have your travel insurance certificate ready as well. You may need it to receive medical care abroad, and it's a good idea to save your insurer's emergency contact number too.

  • Passport (confirm at least 6 months of validity)
  • Passport copy (stored separately)
  • Visa or electronic travel authorization (ESTA, eTA, etc.)
  • Flight booking confirmation
  • Hotel/accommodation booking confirmation
  • Travel insurance certificate and emergency contacts
  • International driving permit (if renting a car)
  • 2 passport photos (in case your passport is lost)

Clothing: Pack for the Weather and Your Itinerary

Clothing takes up the biggest share of your luggage, and it's also the category people over-pack the most. The key is layering. It's the most effective way to handle a range of weather and situations while keeping your bag light.

For basic clothing, plan on roughly one outfit per day, but for trips of a week or more, pack just four or five outfits and do laundry along the way. Using a hotel or Airbnb laundry service, or a coin laundromat, can dramatically cut down your load. Bring underwear and socks to match the number of days, but choose thin, quick-drying fabrics so they dry fast after washing.

Don't overlook weather-specific items either. For a summer trip, bring a hat and sunglasses for sun protection, plus breathable linen clothing. For a winter trip, pack thermal underwear, a padded or down jacket, gloves, and a scarf. If you're visiting somewhere hot but with strong indoor air conditioning, like Southeast Asia, a light long-sleeved cardigan or thin jacket is a must.

For shoes, start with a comfortable pair of sneakers for all the walking, and add one clean pair for occasions that call for something more formal. For a beach trip, you'll also want sandals or flip-flops. Shoes are heavy and take up a big share of your bag, so try not to bring more than two or three pairs.

Pro tip: Rolling your clothes instead of folding them reduces wrinkles and saves space. With compression bags, you can shrink the volume by up to 50%!

Toiletries and Medications: Mind the Carry-On Rules

Toiletries and medications are small, but going without them can cause big headaches. In particular, you need to know the carry-on rules. Most airlines require liquids to be in containers of 100ml or less, placed together in a clear zip-top bag of one liter or less, to be allowed in your carry-on.

Basic toiletries include toothpaste, a toothbrush, shampoo, conditioner, body wash, facial cleanser, moisturizer, and sunscreen. For the liquids among these, prepare travel-size minis (100ml or less) or decant them into travel containers. Solid-format products like shampoo bars and cleansing bars have become popular lately—they're free from liquid restrictions and compact, making them a great fit for travel.

Medications vary from person to person, but there are basics everyone should pack. Pain relievers (such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen), digestive aids, anti-diarrheal medication (such as loperamide), motion sickness pills, bandages, antiseptic, and allergy medicine (antihistamines) are all essential for international travel. In regions like Southeast Asia or India where the water can upset your stomach, be sure to bring anti-diarrheal medication. If you take any prescription drugs, bring enough to last the trip along with an English-language prescription.

Electronics: From Adapters to Power Banks

In modern travel, electronics are indispensable. Your smartphone is used for nearly every aspect of a trip—maps, translation, confirming reservations, taking photos, social media, and more. But if you forget the accessories that go with your devices, you'll run into serious inconvenience.

A universal adapter is an absolute must for international travel. Outlet standards differ from country to country, so if you're visiting several countries, be sure to bring a universal adapter. Choosing one with built-in USB ports lets you charge several devices at once without separate chargers—very convenient. Japan uses the same 110V as Korea but with a different plug shape, while Europe uses 220V with round plugs.

A power bank is a lifeline when you're out sightseeing all day. We recommend a capacity of at least 10,000mAh; a 20,000mAh bank can fully charge a smartphone four or five times, so you'll have peace of mind. Just remember that power banks must go in your carry-on—they cannot be placed in checked luggage. Airlines have their own capacity limits, so check in advance.

A pocket Wi-Fi device or an international roaming eSIM matters too. Without an internet connection abroad, you can't use maps, translation, or search. Before you leave, rent a pocket Wi-Fi device or buy and set up an eSIM. eSIMs have become popular with many travelers lately for being convenient and good value. Set it up before departure so you can get online the moment you land and use it straight from the airport.

  • Smartphone charger and cable
  • Universal adapter (check your destination's outlet standard)
  • Power bank (10,000mAh or more, carry-on)
  • Earphones/headphones
  • Camera and memory card (optional)
  • Pocket Wi-Fi or eSIM
  • Laptop/tablet (if needed)

Other Essentials: The Small but Precious Things Easy to Miss

Beyond the major categories above, there are small but important items that make your trip far more convenient. These are often the things people put off with "I'll grab that later" and then forget, so it's best to add them to your checklist ahead of time.

Cash and cards: Surprisingly many places abroad don't accept card payments. Traditional markets, street food stalls, and small shops in particular tend to need cash. Exchange a small amount of local currency in advance and withdraw the rest from local ATMs, which often gives you a more favorable rate. Choose a credit card with low overseas transaction fees, and check before your trip that it's set up for international use. Just in case, we recommend keeping two or more cards in different places.

Transit cards: Preparing a local transit card in advance is convenient—such as Japan's Suica/PASMO, London's Oyster Card, or Hong Kong's Octopus Card. Lately it's increasingly possible to add local transit cards to Apple Pay or Google Pay. A transit card spares you the hassle of buying a ticket every time and can earn you discounts too.

A padlock: If you plan to stay in hostels, bring a small padlock. Most hostels provide personal lockers, but you often have to bring your own lock. Choosing a TSA-approved lock lets you use it without issues during luggage inspection when entering the United States.

Tips for Splitting Your Carry-On and Checked Bags

Once everything is packed, it's time to split your belongings efficiently between your carry-on and checked bag. How well you do this makes a big difference in your convenience at the airport and your comfort during the flight.

What goes in your carry-on are the things you need during the flight and the things that can't be replaced if lost. That means your passport, wallet, smartphone, power bank, medications, one change of clothes, travel-size toiletries, snacks, entertainment (a book, tablet, earphones), a neck pillow, an eye mask, and so on. Above all, never put your passport and valuables in your checked bag. The key is to pack your carry-on so that you can handle the basics even if your checked luggage goes missing.

Your checked bag holds the rest of your clothing, full-size toiletries, shoes, and other items. Be careful not to exceed the weight limit (usually 23kg), and protect fragile items by wrapping them among your clothes. Leaving some spare room for shopping on the way home is another important tip. Packing a foldable spare bag gives you an extra bag to hold your purchases on the return trip—very handy.

Pro tip: Once you've finished packing, take a photo of your bag. If your luggage goes missing, you can give the airline an accurate description of the bag, which improves your chances of getting it back.

Wrapping Up: Great Packing Is the Start of a Great Trip

Packing is the first step of any trip, and an important process that shapes the comfort of the whole journey. With this checklist, you'll be able to pack efficiently without leaving anything behind. Make it a habit to check once two or three days before departure and once more the day before, and you'll be all set.
