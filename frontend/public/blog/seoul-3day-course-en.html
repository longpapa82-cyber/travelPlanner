Seoul in 3 Days: How to Cover Palaces, the Han River, and Trendy Neighborhoods One Day at a Time

2026-07-30 | Hoonjae Park · myTravel Travel Content Team

Seoul: Why 3 Days Is Just Right

Seoul is a city where 600-year-old palaces, forests of glass towers, the Han River, and alleyway cafes are all jumbled together within a subway stop or two. Having so much to see is a good thing, but the moment you sit down to plan, the list of places you want to visit—Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, Myeongdong, Namsan Tower, Hongdae, Seongsu—just keeps growing, and that can leave you feeling stuck.

Three days is the ideal length for comfortably covering Seoul's three most distinct areas. The most reliable structure is to spend the first day in the palace district centered on Gyeongbokgung, the second day in the downtown and Han River area that runs through Myeongdong, Namsan, and the river, and the third day in the trendy neighborhoods like Hongdae, Yeonnam, and Seongsu. If you get greedy and hop between the north and south of the river in a single day, you'll spend all your time on the subway, so the key is to bundle nearby areas together.

This article is organized around daily routes grouped by area so that a first-time visitor to Seoul can follow it as is. The structure works whether you arrive through Incheon or Gimpo airport.

Day 1: The Palace District—Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, Samcheong-dong, Insa-dong

It depends on your arrival time, but assuming you land in the morning, a good order for the first day is hotel check-in → Gyeongbokgung → Bukchon Hanok Village → Samcheong-dong → Insa-dong. All four spots are within walking distance of one another, so you can take them all in comfortably in a single day.

From the Airport to the City

  • Incheon Airport: The AREX express train reaches Seoul Station in about 43 minutes, or about 60 minutes on the all-stop train. From Seoul Station, transferring to the subway makes any part of the city easy to reach.
  • Gimpo Airport: It's close to the city, so subway lines 5 and 9 or the airport railroad get you downtown in 20–40 minutes. The fares are cheap, too.

Gyeongbokgung

As the main palace of the Joseon dynasty, Gyeongbokgung is something of a symbol of a first trip to Seoul. In front of Gwanghwamun, the Royal Guard Changing Ceremony is held twice a day (usually 10 a.m. and 2 p.m., closed on Tuesdays) and is well worth watching. Admission to the palace is free if you wear hanbok, so many travelers rent hanbok nearby to take photos. It's large, so set aside an hour or two centered on Geunjeongjeon and Gyeonghoeru.

Bukchon Hanok Village and Samcheong-dong

Bukchon Hanok Village, set on the hillside between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung, is a neighborhood of hanok where people actually live, so it takes some consideration to walk quietly and avoid disturbing residents' daily life when taking photos. Come down the alleys and you reach Samcheong-dong, a great place to take a break with its cluster of charming galleries and cafes.

Insa-dong

Insa-dong, with its traditional crafts, calligraphy, and tea houses, is a good place to combine evening gift shopping with dinner. Wrap up the first day at a relaxed pace with a Korean set-menu meal or a traditional tea house tucked into the alleys.

Day 2: Myeongdong, Namsan, N Seoul Tower, and the Han River

On the second day, you walk through Myeongdong, Namsan, and the Han River bundled into one line that represents the energy and night views of downtown Seoul. The natural flow is shopping in Myeongdong in the morning, Namsan and N Seoul Tower in the daytime, and the Han River in the evening.

Morning: Myeongdong

Packed with cosmetics shops, drugstores, and street food, Myeongdong is Seoul's signature shopping street. In the evening, street food stalls line up where you can try snacks like tornado potatoes, egg bread, and street tteokbokki. The nearby Myeongdong Cathedral is also worth a quick look.

Daytime: Namsan and N Seoul Tower

Walking up from Myeongdong or taking the Namsan cable car brings you to N Seoul Tower at the top of Namsan. It's the signature viewpoint for taking in the Seoul skyline at a glance, and the love locks that couples have fastened there are a sight in themselves. If you like to walk, you can add a stroll along the Namsan trail.

Evening: Han River Park

Spread out a mat at Han River Park in Yeouido or Banpo and enjoy dinner with fried chicken and instant noodles. Instant noodles cooked right there at the convenience store and delivered chicken are the signature way Seoulites picnic by the Han River. In the season when the Moonlight Rainbow Fountain on Banpo Bridge is running, the night view is even more special.

Day 3: The Trendy Route—Hongdae, Yeonnam-dong, Seongsu

On the third day, you make the rounds of trendy neighborhoods like Hongdae, Yeonnam-dong, and Seongsu that show Seoul as it is right now. With cafes, select shops, and street performances full of youthful sensibility, they're a great way to fill your last day with energy.

Morning: Hongdae

The Hongdae area, which grew up in front of the art school, is a hub of distinctive shops and busking (street performances). On weekend afternoons, you can often catch indie bands and dance performances on Hongdae's Walking Street.

Daytime: Yeonnam-dong

Yeonnam-dong, which extends from Hongdae, is lined with stylish cafes and small eateries along Gyeongui Line Forest Park (Yeontral Park), where the old Gyeongui railway was turned into a park. It's a neighborhood perfect for walking as if on a stroll and grabbing a cup of coffee.

Afternoon: Seongsu-dong

Seongsu-dong, packed with cafes and pop-up stores made over from old factories and warehouses, is counted as the hippest neighborhood in Seoul these days. Fill up on the mood of your last day while browsing large cafes with an industrial atmosphere and brand pop-up stores. Since it's across the river from Hongdae and Yeonnam, it helps to calculate your subway Line 2 travel time in advance.

The Last Day: Morning Shopping and Departure

Many travelers either add another day to the 3-day itinerary, or, timing it to the third day's flight, wrap up with morning shopping followed by heading to the airport. Avoid traveling far while lugging heavy bags, and it's best to plan your final activities somewhere close to your accommodation or the airport route.

Recommended Shopping Spots

  • Myeongdong and Dongdaemun: Great for buying cosmetics, clothing, and sundries as gifts all in one place. If you're a foreign visitor, be sure to ask about immediate tax refund at the store.
  • Large supermarkets and convenience stores: Good for buying popular food gifts like gim (seaweed), snacks, and instant noodles.
  • Seoul Station and airport shops: If you want to save time right up to the last moment, buying gifts at shops inside Seoul Station or the airport is one option.

Airport Arrival Time

For international flights, arriving at the airport 2–3 hours before departure is safe. Tax refunds, checking baggage, and security screening take more time than you'd think, so give yourself some margin. Because Incheon Airport is far from the city, you need to factor in the airport railroad travel time (about 43–60 minutes from Seoul Station) on top of that.

Seoul Transport and Budget Guide

The thing that most confuses first-time visitors to Seoul is the subway. With so many lines it looks complicated, but if you just put your origin and destination into a map app, it guides you through transfers and fares too, so there's no need to worry much.

Transit Cards

  • T-money (T-money transit card): A rechargeable card that works on the subway, buses, taxis, and even convenience stores, making it the most convenient option. You can easily buy one at subway stations or convenience stores, and if it's your first time in Seoul, this one card is enough.
  • Transfer discounts: When you switch between the subway and buses within a set time, a transfer discount applies. It's especially advantageous for itineraries with multiple trips on a single card.

Rough 3-Day Budget (Per Person)

  • Accommodation: 120,000–350,000 won for 2 nights (guesthouse to business hotel)
  • Food: 30,000–60,000 won per day
  • Transport: 5,000–8,000 won per day
  • Admission and shopping: Varies a lot by person, but budget 50,000–150,000 won to be comfortable. Palace admission is cheap, on the order of a few thousand won.
