Amsterdam 4-Day First-Trip Itinerary: Canals, Museums, and a Countryside Windmill Village Route

2026-07-30 | Hoonjae Park · myTravel Travel Content Team

Your First Amsterdam: Why 4 Days Is Just Right

Amsterdam is a compact city, so its core areas sit within walking distance of one another. From the narrow, tall houses lined up along the canals to its world-class museums and the distinctive street culture where bicycles take priority over cars, even a few days is enough to feel the character of the city.

Four days is the ideal length to comfortably cover central Amsterdam plus one nearby destination. The most stable structure is to use the first day for travel and a stroll around the canals, split the second and third days between museums and the city and countryside, and leave the last day relaxed in the morning before heading to the airport. Cramming museums into a single day quickly wears you out, so the key is to spread them across one or two per day.

This article focuses on daily routes grouped by area and theme so that a first-time Amsterdam visitor can follow along directly. Passport holders from many countries can stay visa-free for up to 90 days, so with a good plan you can visit without much hassle.

Day 1: Airport Arrival and the Canals and Jordaan District

It depends on your arrival time, but assuming you land in the afternoon, it's best to keep the first day light, going in the order of hotel check-in → canal stroll → Jordaan district.

From the Airport to the City

  • Schiphol Airport → Central Station: There's a train station right below the airport, so you can reach Amsterdam Central Station in about 15 to 20 minutes. The good access to the city center keeps first-day travel easy.
  • City transport: Trams, buses, and the metro are well connected, and you can ride them conveniently with a GVB public transport card or a 1 to 7 day pass. The city center is great for getting around on foot or by bicycle.

Canal Stroll

Amsterdam's canal district is a symbol of the city, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Walking along the semicircular canals that flow through Herengracht, Keizersgracht, and Prinsengracht, you'll capture a picture-perfect scene wherever you take a photo. If you have time to spare, a canal boat tour that lets you view the city from the water suits the first day well.

The Jordaan District

West of the canals, the Jordaan district is a charming neighborhood packed with narrow alleys, cozy cafes, galleries, and vintage shops. It's a lovely place to wrap up the first day with a beer at a brown cafe (a traditional pub) favored by locals.

Day 2: The Museum Quarter (Van Gogh Museum and Rijksmuseum)

On the second day, you'll tour museums centered on the Museumplein (Museum Square) area, Amsterdam's pride. The Van Gogh Museum and the national Rijksmuseum face each other across the square, so you can walk back and forth between them.

Morning: The Van Gogh Museum

This museum holds the world's largest collection of works by Vincent van Gogh. Following the chronologically arranged exhibition, you can read the artist's life and the evolution of his brushwork at a glance. Online advance booking is effectively essential, and it runs on timed-entry tickets, so reserve your preferred time slot in advance.

Afternoon: The Rijksmuseum (National Museum)

This is the Netherlands' flagship museum, where you can encounter Rembrandt's The Night Watch and Vermeer's masterpieces. It's large, so deciding which galleries interest you in advance saves your energy. Here too, online booking helps reduce the wait in line.

Evening: Museum Square and De Pijp

Rest a while on the square's lawn, then head south to the De Pijp neighborhood and enjoy dinner around the Albert Cuyp Market. With restaurants of many nationalities and casual bars gathered here, it's a great spot to end the day.

  • Viewing tip: Museums are relatively quiet right after they open in the morning. To see two large museums in one day, allow 2 to 3 hours for each and slip in a rest on the square in between.

Day 3: The Anne Frank House and Touring the City by Bicycle

The third day is a day to feel both the city's history and its daily life. In the morning, you'll visit the Anne Frank House, and in the afternoon, you'll tour the city by bicycle, the quintessentially Amsterdam way.

Morning: The Anne Frank House

This is the place that preserves, just as it was, the hidden annex where the Anne Frank family lived in hiding during World War II. Walking through the narrow stairs and the space behind the rotating bookcase, facing the story from the diary firsthand, leaves a deep impression. Tickets are sold only through an online booking system, so advance reservation is absolutely required, and it's very popular, so it's best to book it first as soon as your dates are set.

Afternoon: A Loop Around the City by Bicycle

Amsterdam is a cyclist's paradise where bicycles take priority over cars. Dedicated bike lanes are laid out densely across the city, so pedaling along to connect canals, bridges, and parks is the most local way to enjoy this city. There are rental shops all over town, so borrow one for about half a day and tour from Vondelpark to the canal alleys.

Day 4: The Nearby Zaanse Schans Windmill Village and Departure

On the last day, if your flight time allows, we recommend a structure where you visit the nearby Zaanse Schans windmill village in the morning and then head to the airport. If time is tight, you could skip the countryside and wrap up with some light shopping in the city instead.

The Zaanse Schans Windmill Village

It's an open-air village reachable in around 20 minutes by train from Central Station, then a walk or short trip from a nearby station. The windmills that come to mind when you think of the Netherlands line the riverside, and there are workshops gathered together where you can watch traditional wooden clogs and cheese being made. Going early in the morning lets you enjoy the scenery in peace.

  • Scheduling tip: Whether to include the countryside depends on your flight time on the last day. If it's a late-afternoon flight, a morning countryside trip is perfectly feasible; if it's a morning or early-afternoon flight, don't push it and swap in city shopping instead.
  • Souvenirs: Cheese, wooden clog miniatures, and tulip souvenirs are all good to browse together at the windmill village or the city markets.

Airport Arrival Time

For international flights, arriving at the airport 2 to 3 hours before departure is safe. Schiphol Airport is large, and security screening and tax refunds take time, so if you're visiting the countryside, calculate plenty of travel time.

Amsterdam Transport and Budget Guide

Central Amsterdam is great to get around on foot, and adding a tram or bicycle when needed solves most of your routes. When routes get confusing, just enter your origin and destination in the Google Maps app and it will guide you through tram and train transfers and even fares, so there's no need to worry much.

Transport Passes

  • GVB public transport card: Convenient because you can ride the city's trams, buses, and metro with unlimited use on a timed pass (1 to 7 days). It pays off if you stay several days and ride the tram often.
  • OV-chipkaart (rechargeable): A transport card you can use broadly, including on regional trains, which is useful for countryside trips like Zaanse Schans.
  • Bicycle rental: You can rent by the half-day or full day at rental shops all over town. It's a way of getting around that lets you truly feel the local vibe.

Rough 4-Day Budget (Per Person)

  • Airfare: Varies by season, roughly USD 600 to 1,100 (a big difference between direct and connecting flights)
  • Accommodation: About USD 300 to 700 for 3 nights (hostel to mid-range hotel)
  • Food: 40 to 70 euros per day
  • Transport: 8 to 15 euros per day (including tram and bicycle)
  • Museum admission: Major museums like the Van Gogh Museum, Rijksmuseum, and Anne Frank House are around 20 euros each

Amsterdam's currency is the euro (EUR), and since the felt cost changes with the exchange rate, it's a good idea to check the euro rate before you leave and set your budget accordingly.
