Prague 4-Day First Trip Itinerary: Old Town, Charles Bridge, and Prague Castle Walking Route

2026-07-30 | myTravel Team

Your First Prague: Why 4 Days Is Just Right

Prague is often called 'the most beautiful city in Europe.' From the Old Town with its waves of red rooftops, to Charles Bridge crossing the Vltava River, to Prague Castle perched on the hill, the entire city feels like one open-air museum. But when you actually sit down to plan, it can be overwhelming to figure out the most efficient order to visit the Old Town, the castle, the Jewish Quarter, and Mala Strana.

Four days is the ideal length for comfortably covering Prague's Old Town and the castle area. Prague's main sights are clustered closely enough on either side of the Vltava to reach on foot, so the key is to divide your route between the east side of the river (Old Town) and the west side (the castle district). The most reliable structure is: transfer and Old Town on day one, cross Charles Bridge to Prague Castle on day two, Mala Strana and the Jewish Quarter on day three, and a morning stroll before departure on the last day.

This article is organized around daily walking routes grouped by which side of the river you're on, so a first-time Prague traveler can follow along directly. One of Prague's biggest advantages is that most sights are within walking distance, so public transit is rarely a burden.

Day 1: Airport Arrival and Old Town

It depends on your arrival time, but assuming you land in the afternoon, it's best to keep the first day light with hotel check-in → Old Town Square → the Astronomical Clock → Tyn Church. The Old Town sights are all connected on foot, centered on the square.

From the Airport to the City

  • Vaclav Havel Airport: There's no direct metro line to the city center, so you take a bus plus metro or the airport bus.
  • Bus + Metro: Take bus 119 to Nadrazi Veleslavin station, then transfer to metro line A (green) to reach the city center. This takes about 30 to 45 minutes.
  • Airport Express (AE): This line runs directly to Prague's main train station, which is convenient on the first day when you have a lot of luggage and don't want to transfer.

Old Town Square

This is the heart of a Prague trip. Pastel-toned old buildings surround the square, and depending on the season, open-air markets or performances are held here. Sit on a bench in the square and take in the city's atmosphere first.

The Astronomical Clock and Tyn Church

The Astronomical Clock mounted on the Old Town Hall wall puts on a show every hour on the hour, with apostle figures passing by the windows, so crowds gather just before the hour. Across the square, Tyn Church with its two striking pointed spires is a symbol of Prague. Dinner is a great way to wrap up the day at a restaurant around the square with Czech koleno (pork knuckle) or goulash.

Day 2: Charles Bridge and Prague Castle

On day two you walk the highlights of Prague grouped along one axis: Charles Bridge → Prague Castle → St. Vitus Cathedral. It's a route that connects comfortably on foot from the Old Town, across the river, up the hill to the castle.

Early Morning: Charles Bridge

Crossing the Vltava River, Charles Bridge is a 600-year-old bridge lined with 30 statues of saints. During the day it's packed shoulder to shoulder with tourists, vendors, and street artists, so crossing early in the morning lets you fully enjoy the quiet bridge and the mist-covered river. If you want to capture photos, the morning hours are strongly recommended.

Morning to Afternoon: Prague Castle

Considered one of the largest ancient castle complexes in the world, Prague Castle is an entire hillside that is one castle. Admission tickets are divided by the area you visit, so it's good to decide in advance which ticket type fits the places you want to see, such as St. Vitus Cathedral, the Old Royal Palace, and Golden Lane. You can also watch the changing of the guard at the castle's main gate.

St. Vitus Cathedral

This is a Gothic cathedral located inside Prague Castle. Its soaring spires and stained glass are overwhelming, and it's the one place inside the castle you least want to miss.

  • Route tip: On the way down from the castle, if you follow the winding stairway path toward Mala Strana, you can look out over the city panorama beyond the red rooftops.

Day 3: Mala Strana, Petrin Hill, and the Jewish Quarter

On day three you explore the charming Mala Strana and Petrin Hill on the west side of the river, then cross back over to the Jewish Quarter. By day three, you'll have gotten used to Prague's walking rhythm, making the backstreet wandering even more enjoyable.

Morning: Mala Strana

This is a Baroque-style neighborhood spread out below Prague Castle. Narrow lanes, pastel buildings, and charming shops make it lovely for a stroll. The alley with a traffic light known as 'the narrowest street in Europe' is nearby too, adding a small, fun sight.

Midday: Petrin Hill

A hilltop park behind Mala Strana that you can reach by funicular or on foot. At the top is the Petrin Lookout Tower, which looks like a scaled-down Eiffel Tower, making it a great viewpoint to take in the panorama of the city at a glance.

Afternoon: The Jewish Quarter (Josefov)

Cross back over the river and head north of the Old Town to reach the Jewish Quarter. One of the oldest synagogues in Europe and an old cemetery with layers of graves reveal another side of the city's history. The quiet, contemplative atmosphere has a different texture from the previous two days.

Day 4: Morning Stroll and Departure

On the last day, wrap up with a morning stroll and then the transfer to the airport, timed around your flight. Avoid traveling far with heavy luggage, and plan your final activities somewhere close to your hotel or airport route.

Recommended Final Route

  • Wenceslas Square: A wide boulevard in the New Town lined with shops and cafes, great for combining last-minute shopping and a walk.
  • Powder Tower and Municipal House: Located on the boundary between the Old Town and the New Town, they're easy to browse when you have only a little time left.
  • Souvenirs: Bohemian crystal, marionette dolls, and Czech beer-related items are classic Prague gifts.

Airport Arrival Time

For international flights, arriving at the airport 2 to 3 hours before departure is safe. Vaclav Havel Airport has no direct metro line, so you'll need to take a bus or the airport bus—allow plenty of time for luggage and travel. From the city to the airport takes about 30 to 45 minutes depending on traffic.

Prague Transit and Budget Guide

Because Prague's main sights are clustered within walking distance, you won't need to ride public transit much. That said, trams, the metro, and buses are useful for airport transfers or getting up the hill.

Transit Tickets

  • Paper tickets and mobile app tickets: Prague public transit uses time-based tickets (e.g., 30-minute or 90-minute passes), which you can use to transfer between metro, tram, and bus within the set time. Paper tickets must be validated in the validation machine to be valid.
  • Tram sightseeing: Certain tram lines pass along the riverside and below the castle, so the view out the window alone becomes sightseeing. It's great to use when your legs get tired.
  • Walking-centered: From the Old Town across Charles Bridge to below the castle connects comfortably on foot, so there's no need to force yourself onto public transit.

Currency Note

The Czech Republic uses the Czech koruna (CZK), not the euro. Some shops in tourist areas will accept euros, but the exchange rate is often unfavorable, so paying in koruna or having koruna cash on hand is to your advantage. Travelers from many countries can stay up to 90 days without a visa.

Rough 4-Day Budget (Per Person)

  • Airfare: Varies widely depending on direct vs. connecting flights and the season. Peak season and direct flights are more expensive.
  • Accommodation: For 3 nights, the closer to the Old Town the more expensive, while guesthouses on the outskirts are cheaper.
  • Food: Around 200 to 400 koruna per meal at local restaurants; the tourist-center areas are pricier.
  • Transit: Since it's mostly walking, on many days one or two tickets a day is enough.
  • Admission: Varies by what you visit, such as Prague Castle area tickets and the lookout tower.

Because your felt cost changes with the exchange rate, it's best to check the koruna rate before departure when setting your budget.
