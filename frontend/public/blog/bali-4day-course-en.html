Bali 4-Day Itinerary: Balancing Ubud Culture and the Southern Beaches

2026-07-30 | myTravel Team

Bali: Why 4 Days Strikes a Good Balance

Bali is not an island with just one face. The inland culture of Ubud, lined with rice terraces and temples; the southern beaches where surfboards bob in the waves; and Uluwatu Temple, where you catch the sunset atop a cliff — regions with completely different characters are gathered on a single island. That is why it feels like a shame to only relax or only sightsee here.

Four days is a good length to comfortably split these two experiences. The key is to divide your time between the inland (Ubud) and the south (the beaches), two days each, because the two regions are more than an hour apart by car, so going back and forth in a single day means spending all your time in transit. For that reason, this article organizes the route around grouping regions together.

Bali has almost no public transportation, so getting around is mostly done by chartered car or scooter. Ride-hailing apps like Grab and Gojek are also used, but some areas such as Ubud have restrictions, so it is a good idea to check with your accommodation or driver in advance. As long as you keep this in mind, it is an island that is easy enough even for a first trip to Southeast Asia.

Day 1: Arrival at the Airport and Easing Into the Southern Beaches

Most flights arrive at Ngurah Rai (Denpasar) International Airport. Since the airport is in the south, a good plan for the first day is to not overdo it and instead ease in at the southern beaches near the airport.

Entry and Visas

  • Visa on Arrival (VOA): Depending on your nationality, you may be able to get a visa on arrival at the airport or enter visa-free under certain conditions. Policies can change, so be sure to check Indonesia's immigration rules before you depart.
  • From the airport into town: It is about 15 to 40 minutes by car to Kuta and Seminyak in the south. Using the official taxi counter or a ride-hailing app helps you avoid being overcharged.

Kuta

This is the closest well-known beach to the airport. Thanks to its wide white sand and gentle waves, it is popular with beginner surfers, and board rentals and lessons are easy to find. With shops and restaurants clustered nearby, it is a convenient place to settle in on your first day.

Evening: Seminyak

Seminyak, just north of Kuta, is home to stylish cafes and restaurants. A beachfront beach club is a great spot to watch the sunset over dinner. Since this area is not one that raises big jet-lag concerns on the first day, take it easy, soak in the sea, and wrap up the day gently.

Day 2: Canggu Surfing and a Day at the Southern Beaches

On the second day, stay in the south and enjoy surfing and beach clubs centered on Canggu. The southern beach areas are close to one another, so grouping them into a single day is efficient for travel.

Morning: Surfing in Canggu

Canggu is a beach town beloved by surfers and nomads. It has a wide spectrum from beginner lessons to intermediate-and-up breaks, so if you want to properly learn to surf, it is a good idea to book a morning lesson. The waves tend to be relatively calm in the morning.

Afternoon: Cafes and Beach Clubs

Canggu has many brunch cafes and distinctive shops, making it a great place to rest after surfing. In the afternoon, spend your time swimming and relaxing at a beach club by the sea. By Bali standards, beach clubs are somewhat pricey, but for a day devoted to relaxation they are worth it.

Evening: Seafood and Sunset

The beachfront in Jimbaran to the south is famous for its seafood grill restaurants. Watching the sunset over dinner at a table set out on the sand is one of the signature scenes of a trip to southern Bali.

  • Travel tip: Roads in the southern beach areas get congested often. When traveling between Canggu, Seminyak, and Jimbaran, factor in daytime traffic and give yourself plenty of time.

Day 3: Ubud Culture and the Rice Terraces

On the third day, leave the south and head inland to Ubud. It takes about 1 to 1.5 hours by car from the south to Ubud, so it is best to set out early in the morning. If possible, we recommend booking a night's stay in Ubud starting this day to reduce round-trip travel.

Morning: Tegalalang Rice Terraces

Tegalalang, north of Ubud, is famous for its rice terraces, where waves of green rise layer upon layer. Lit by the morning sun, the terraces are one of Bali's iconic landscapes and a great spot for photos.

Afternoon: Monkey Forest and Ubud Market

  • Monkey Forest: A sacred forest home to wild monkeys. They are cute, but they go after food and belongings, so keep a careful eye on glasses, hats, plastic bags, and the like.
  • Ubud Market: A traditional market where you can buy Balinese handicrafts such as rattan bags, woodcrafts, and batik fabric. Haggling is standard, so take your time browsing.

Evening: The Atmosphere of Ubud

Central Ubud is dotted with traditional Balinese dance venues and atmospheric restaurants. It is a great place for a calm, cultural evening that is quite different from the liveliness of the south.

Day 4: Uluwatu Sunset and Departure

On the last day, plan around your flight time. In the morning, head back from Ubud toward the south and stop by places you have not yet seen; and if your flight departs in the late afternoon, wrapping up the trip with the sunset and Kecak dance at Uluwatu Temple makes for the most memorable finish.

Uluwatu Temple

A Hindu temple perched atop a cliff at the southern tip. The sunset against the backdrop of the Indian Ocean is spectacular, so a late-afternoon visit is best. There are many monkeys here too, so mind your belongings.

Kecak Dance

Around sunset, the performance venue beside the temple hosts the Kecak dance, performed to the "chak-chak" chanting of a male chorus. Set against the sunset, it is more than worthy of crowning the last night of a Bali trip. It is very popular, so it is best to secure seats in advance.

Temple Etiquette and Getting to the Airport

  • Wearing a sarong: At Balinese temples, wearing a sarong to cover the lower body is standard. Most places lend them for free at the entrance, so follow the rules when you enter.
  • Getting to the airport: It is about 40 minutes to 1 hour by car from Uluwatu to the airport. For international flights it is safest to arrive 2 to 3 hours before departure, so carefully calculate the time the performance ends against your flight time.

Bali Transport and Budget Guide

Because Bali has virtually no public transportation, planning how you will get around is half the trip. Setting your regional transport methods and a rough budget in advance makes things much easier.

Getting Around

  • Chartered car (with driver): Renting a car and driver by the day is the most common approach. It is convenient on days with a lot of inter-regional travel, like Ubud to the south.
  • Scooter: Cheap and freeing, but the road conditions are rough and there is a risk of accidents. Be sure to have an international driving permit, a helmet, and travel insurance.
  • Ride-hailing apps: Grab and Gojek are widely used in the south. Note that some areas, such as Ubud, have restrictions on their use, so check the local situation.

Seasons

Bali is broadly divided into the dry season (roughly April to October) and the wet season (roughly November to March). The dry season is more favorable for outdoor activities and the beach, while the wet season brings frequent squally rain. That said, even in the wet season it does not rain all day, so mixing in indoor and cultural activities works well.

Rough 4-Day Budget (Per Person)

  • Airfare: Varies widely by season and whether direct or connecting; expect a broad range depending on those factors.
  • Accommodation: For 3 nights, the range is wide — from guesthouses to pool villas.
  • Food: Local eateries are cheap, while beach clubs and famous restaurants are pricey. Budget for a moderate daily amount.
  • Transport (chartered car, etc.): A moderate daily amount.
  • Admission, activities, and shopping: Surf lessons, performances, souvenirs, and the like vary a lot by person, so leave a comfortable margin.
