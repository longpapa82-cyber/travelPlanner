The Travel Apps You Actually Need: Maps, Translation, Currency, and Planning

2026-07-10 | myTravel Team

Start with the Right Apps, Not the Most Apps

Open any travel forum and you'll find lists of "50 must-have apps" for your trip. Install all of them and your phone turns into a cluttered mess of icons you'll never open twice. The truth is that most travelers rely on a small, dependable set of tools that cover a few essential jobs: finding your way, understanding what people are saying, knowing how much you're actually paying, and keeping your plans in one place.

This guide is organized by what the app does for you, not by brand hype. For each category, we'll cover what to look for, which options are worth your storage space, and the small habits that make these apps genuinely useful once you land. Whether you're heading somewhere for a long weekend or a month-long journey, the categories below cover the parts that trip people up most often.

A quick note before you download anything: do it on Wi-Fi before you leave home. App stores are slow (or blocked) on some airport and hotel networks, and offline data packs for maps and translation can be hundreds of megabytes. Setting up in advance saves you from fumbling with a weak signal when you actually need the tools working.

Maps and Offline Navigation

Maps are the one category no traveler should skip. But the app you use at home isn't automatically the right one abroad. The single biggest upgrade is offline maps: downloading a region while you have Wi-Fi so it keeps working when your data runs dry or you step into a subway dead zone.

What to look for

  • Downloadable regions: The ability to save a whole city or country for offline use, including search and turn-by-turn directions.
  • Transit coverage: Reliable subway, bus, and train routing for the specific city you're visiting. Coverage varies wildly by region.
  • Walking accuracy: Pedestrian directions that account for stairs, alleys, and pedestrian-only zones, which matters in dense old-town areas.

How the main options compare

The mainstream mapping apps handle offline downloads, transit, and points of interest well in most large cities, and their live traffic data is excellent. Dedicated offline-first apps built from open map data tend to shine for hiking, rural areas, and countries where the big players have thinner coverage. Many experienced travelers keep one of each and switch depending on where they are.

Pin your itinerary stops in advance. When you build a trip in myTravel, each day's activities include a location. Before you go, drop those same spots as saved pins or favorites in your map app so your daily route is ready offline. It turns a list of place names into a walkable path.

One practical habit: screenshot the walking directions from your accommodation to nearby transit before you head out for the day. Even the best offline map occasionally struggles to reacquire your position underground, and a static image never needs a signal.

Translation That Works Without Signal

You don't need to be fluent to travel well, but a good translation app removes a surprising amount of friction: reading a menu, asking for directions, decoding a train station sign, or explaining a dietary restriction to a waiter.

The features that matter

  • Camera translation: Point your phone at a menu or sign and see the translated text overlaid. This is the feature that feels like magic the first time it works on a wall of unfamiliar characters.
  • Offline language packs: Download the languages you need before departure. Camera and text translation then work even in airplane mode.
  • Conversation mode: Two-way spoken translation that lets you and a local take turns talking into the phone. Slower than fluent speech, but it gets you through real exchanges.

A realistic expectation: machine translation is good enough for logistics and everyday needs, but it can garble idioms, humor, and formal or legal phrasing. For anything important, keep sentences short and literal. "Where is the bus to the airport?" translates cleanly. A long, casual sentence with slang often does not.

It also helps to learn five or six phrases by heart before you go: hello, thank you, please, excuse me, and "do you speak English?" in the local language. People respond warmly to the effort, and it buys goodwill that no app can replicate.

Currency and Spending Awareness

Nothing spoils the memory of a great meal like discovering you paid three times what you thought. Currency confusion and hidden fees quietly drain travel budgets, so this category is worth a little attention.

Two different jobs

There are two things to keep straight. The first is conversion: knowing that a price tag of a certain amount in local currency equals a certain amount back home, so you can judge whether something is a good deal in the moment. The second is tracking: keeping a running total of what you've actually spent so the trip doesn't blow past your budget.

  • Conversion apps: A dedicated currency app with a downloadable offline rate lets you check prices instantly without data. Rates drift daily, so refresh on Wi-Fi each morning.
  • Card and fee awareness: Watch for "dynamic currency conversion" at payment terminals and ATMs, where you're offered a charge in your home currency at a poor rate. Almost always, choosing to pay in the local currency is cheaper.
  • Spending logs: A simple expense tracker, or the expense feature inside your planning app, keeps you honest about the daily total.
Log expenses where you already keep your plan. myTravel supports 7 major currencies for trip expenses, so you can record what you spend on food, transport, and activities right alongside your itinerary. Keeping spending in the same place as your plan means fewer apps to juggle and a clearer picture of the trip's real cost.

One more small habit that pays off: photograph or note down the exchange rate you got the first time you take out cash. It gives you a reference point for the rest of the trip and makes it obvious if a later ATM or exchange booth is quietly worse.

Planning and Keeping Your Trip Organized

Maps, translation, and currency solve problems in the moment. But the app that shapes the whole trip is the one where your plan lives. This is where scattered notes, saved links, and half-remembered recommendations either come together into a coherent day, or don't.

What a planning app should do

  • Hold a day-by-day itinerary: A clear structure where each day has its activities, locations, and rough timing, so you're never staring at a blank map wondering what's next.
  • Give you a starting point: The hardest part of planning is the first draft. A tool that suggests a reasonable route through a city saves hours of research and gives you something concrete to adjust.
  • Stay flexible: Plans change. The best planning tool makes it easy to reorder, add, or drop activities as you discover things on the ground.

Once your itinerary exists, the other apps slot in around it: pins in your map app follow the plan, your translation packs match the language, and your currency and expense tracking share the same trip. A good plan is the spine that the rest of your travel toolkit hangs on.

Building Your Personal Travel App Kit

You don't need a folder full of apps. You need one dependable tool in each of the categories above, set up before you leave. Here's a simple routine for assembling your kit and getting it ready.

Before you leave (on Wi-Fi)

  1. Download offline maps for your destination city or region.
  2. Install a translation app and pull down the offline language pack.
  3. Set up a currency app and save the current offline rate.
  4. Build your day-by-day plan in myTravel and review each day's stops.
  5. Pin your itinerary locations in your map app so your route is ready offline.

On the road

  • Refresh currency rates each morning while you have signal.
  • Log expenses as you go, not at the end of the day when you've forgotten half of them.
  • Screenshot key directions before heading into transit dead zones.
  • Adjust your plan as you discover new places, so it stays accurate rather than aspirational.

The goal isn't to be glued to your phone. It's the opposite: a well-prepared kit means you spend less time squinting at screens and more time actually being where you are. Get the essentials set up in advance, keep your plan in one place, and let the tools quietly do their job in the background.
