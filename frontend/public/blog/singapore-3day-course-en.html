Singapore 3-Day First-Timer Itinerary: Routing Marina Bay, Sentosa & Chinatown

2026-07-30 | Hoonjae Park · myTravel Travel Content Team

Your First Singapore Trip: Why 3 Days Is Just Right

Singapore is a small city-state roughly the size of Seoul. Its attractions are packed densely into a compact area, so unlike traveling across a large country, you almost never spend an entire day just getting from place to place. That's why three days is enough to comfortably cover the key areas like Marina Bay, Sentosa, and Chinatown.

The key to a Singapore trip is grouping each day around one area. Since one or two MRT (subway) stops connect most of the major sights, planning one day for Marina Bay, one for Sentosa, and a final half-day for a theme like Chinatown or Little India keeps your route clean.

Korean passport holders can enter visa-free, and the currency is the Singapore dollar (SGD). Prices are on the higher side among Southeast Asian countries, so it's more relaxing to set aside a generous budget for hotels and meals. This article is organized around daily routes grouped by area so first-time visitors can follow along step by step.

Day 1: Airport Arrival and a Night at Marina Bay

It depends on your arrival time, but assuming you land in the afternoon, a good order for the first day is hotel check-in → Merlion Park → Gardens by the Bay. The Marina Bay area really comes into its own at night when the lights come on, so it fits perfectly as a first-evening plan.

From the Airport to the City

  • Changi Airport → city center: The MRT connected directly to the airport takes about 30 minutes to reach downtown. World-famous Changi Airport itself has plenty to see, so you might take a quick look at the indoor garden and the Jewel waterfall before heading out.
  • Transit card: Topping up an EZ-Link card lets you use the MRT and buses conveniently with a single card. Recently, tapping a foreign-issued contactless credit card directly at the gate has also become widely used.

Merlion Park

The Merlion statue, with a lion's head and a fish's body, is the symbol of Singapore. With the Marina Bay Sands hotel framed behind the water-spouting Merlion, it's a great spot for your first Singapore photo.

Gardens by the Bay

This is a futuristic garden lined with giant artificial tree structures called Supertrees. Every evening a free Supertree light show (Garden Rhapsody) is held, and the roughly 15-minute performance, with lights turning on across the trees and music playing, is one of Marina Bay's signature attractions. The paid domes (Flower Dome and Cloud Forest) can be pushed to the next day's plan.

Day 2: A Full Day on Sentosa

On the second day, spend the whole day on Sentosa, the resort island packed with things to do. The trip in itself—by cable car or the Sentosa Express (monorail) from the city—is one of the fun parts.

Morning to Afternoon: Universal Studios

Sentosa's flagship attraction, Universal Studios Singapore, is a theme park of a size that's enjoyable in Asia. Popular attractions can have long waits, so it's advantageous to enter right at the morning opening time. If you'd prefer a more relaxed atmosphere over the rides, the adjacent S.E.A. Aquarium is also a good alternative.

Afternoon: Sentosa Beaches

After leaving the theme park, Siloso and Palawan beaches are great for dipping your feet in and taking a rest. The palm-lined beachfront has plenty of beach bars and cafes, making it ideal for cooling off during a hot afternoon.

Evening: A Stroll Through the Resort District

Once the sun sets, fountain shows and light events are held across Sentosa. If you've played hard all day, have a hearty dinner at a restaurant in the resort district in the evening, then head back to the city by cable car while looking down on the Marina Bay night view.

  • Getting-around tip: Fares and travel times for Sentosa differ depending on how you enter (cable car, monorail, or on foot). Since you'll move around the island several times throughout the day, it helps to check the free and paid shuttle routes within the island in advance.

Day 3: Chinatown, Little India & Clarke Quay

On the last day, group Chinatown, Little India, and Clarke Quay—where Singapore's diverse cultures blend—into a half-day route. All three areas are closely connected by MRT, so you can cover them in a short but full way before your flight.

Morning: Chinatown

This is an area where narrow alleys hung with red lanterns meet the ornate Buddha Tooth Relic Temple. With plenty of souvenir streets and inexpensive food, it's a good place to handle some light shopping and breakfast together on your last day.

Midday: Little India

This is an Indian cultural district full of colorful buildings and the scent of spices. The vivid Hindu temples and murals are great for photos, and you can enjoy a different kind of lunch with local Indian curry and chai.

Midday to Afternoon: Clarke Quay

Lined along the Singapore River, Clarke Quay is famous as a riverside walking course by day and a nightlife spot packed with rooftop bars at night. Taking a river cruise that runs along the river lets you take in the waterfront scenery from Merlion Park to Marina Bay Sands all at once, wrapping up your trip.

How to Eat Cheaply in Singapore: Hawker Centres

Prices in Singapore tend to be high, but thanks to hawker centres (large open-air food courts), food costs at least are something you can save on as much as you like. Since locals use them as part of everyday life, prices are friendly and you can taste dishes from a variety of countries all in one place.

Notable Hawker Centres

  • Lau Pa Sat: A historic hawker centre near Marina Bay, where in the evening the lane fills with satay (grilled skewer) stalls. It's easy to drop by since it's close to the tourist route.
  • Makansutra Gluttons Bay: Set along the Marina Bay waterfront, it's a great place to enjoy local food while taking in the night view.

Local Dishes You Must Try

  • Chicken rice: Singapore's national dish, pairing boiled chicken with savory, flavorful rice.
  • Chilli crab and pepper crab: Crab dishes that are either sweet-and-spicy or full of peppery aroma, great for sharing among several people.
  • Laksa and bak kut teh: Laksa, a spicy noodle dish in coconut broth, and bak kut teh, a pork rib soup rich with pepper, are also special treats.

At hawker centres there's a chope custom of reserving a seat with a tissue packet or personal belongings, so grab a seat first and then order your food.

Singapore Transportation and Budget Guide

Singapore has well-developed public transportation, so it's easy to travel without a rental car. MRT lines pass by most tourist attractions, and if you just enter your origin and destination in the Google Maps app, it guides you through transfers and fares too, so finding your way isn't much of a worry.

Transportation Tips

  • EZ-Link card: A rechargeable transit card that lets you use the MRT and buses with a single card—the most convenient option. You can buy and top it up at convenience stores and stations.
  • Tapping a contactless credit card: Recently, tapping a foreign-issued contactless card directly at the gate has become widely used. For a short trip, this method can be convenient without issuing a separate card.
  • Getting around on foot: In areas where attractions are clustered, like Marina Bay or Chinatown, walking is often actually faster.

Rough 3-Day Budget (per person)

  • Airfare: 400,000–700,000 KRW depending on the season
  • Accommodation: 200,000–500,000 KRW for 2 nights (guesthouse to mid-range hotel; pricier than Tokyo or Bangkok due to high prices)
  • Food: 30–60 SGD per day (you can save by using hawker centres, and it rises if you enjoy dishes like chilli crab)
  • Transportation: 5–10 SGD per day
  • Admission and shopping: Theme park admission such as Universal Studios takes up a large share, so budget generously for it separately

Singapore has relatively high prices even within Southeast Asia, so it's good to check the exchange rate in advance and set your budget with a little room to spare.
