The Art of Budget Travel: 10 Ways to Save Money

2026-02-05 | Hoonjae Park · myTravel Travel Content Team

Do you think you need to be wealthy to travel abroad? That's a big misconception. What matters isn't how much you spend, but how you spend it. Among the long-term travelers exploring every corner of the world, plenty enjoy rich, full trips on surprisingly small budgets. Their secret isn't anything mysterious — it comes down to a little information, some planning, and a flexible mindset.

In this article, we'll walk through 10 proven ways to genuinely cut costs on international travel. From booking flights to lodging, food, sightseeing, transport, and currency exchange, you'll learn concrete strategies for saving money at every stage of a trip. Apply these tips and you can trim your costs by 30–50% for the same destination — and actually improve the quality of your trip in the process. See for yourself that traveling on a budget doesn't mean settling for a lesser experience.

1. Booking Flights on the Cheap

Airfare is the single biggest chunk of most travel budgets. Depending on how you book, the price can swing anywhere from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars. The most basic strategy is to travel in the off-season. Avoiding school breaks and holiday long weekends often cuts airfare by more than half. Flights to Europe, for example, can run over $1,500 in the July–August peak season, but you can find them for under $800 in March–April or October–November.

Taking connecting flights is another smart move. They take longer than direct flights, but they're often 30–40% cheaper. Routing through Southeast Asia can save a lot on flights to Europe, and you can even squeeze in a little sightseeing during your layover — two birds with one stone. Booking early also helps: reserving your ticket two to three months before departure tends to land you the best price. Signing up for airline promotion alerts lets you catch deals as soon as they drop.

Comparing multiple flight search sites matters too. Even for the identical flight, prices can vary depending on where you buy it. Meta-search engines like Skyscanner, Google Flights, and Kayak make it easy to find the cheapest option. Shifting your departure and arrival dates by a day in either direction can reveal surprisingly large price differences, so if your schedule is flexible, it's well worth a try.

2. Saving on Accommodation

Lodging is the second-largest cost after airfare. Considering alternatives to hotels can save you a significant amount. Hostels are the most economical option for solo travelers. A bed in a dormitory (shared room) can run as little as $10–25 a night, and shared kitchens let you cook for yourself, cutting food costs too. Another big plus is the chance to mingle with other travelers in the common areas and pick up local tips.

Airbnb is especially advantageous for groups of two or more. It's cheaper than a hotel, comes with a kitchen to save on meals, and offers the unique experience of living in a local's home. Many listings offer discounts for longer stays (seven nights or more), so take advantage if you're settling into one city for a while. Guesthouses are another excellent option. In Southeast Asia and South America, guesthouses are well-established, and you can find clean, comfortable rooms for $10–20 a night.

Money-saving tip: Weekday stays (Mon–Thu) are on average 20–30% cheaper than weekends (Fri–Sun). If you can be flexible with your dates, lean into weekday stays as much as possible.

3. Eating Like a Local

Eating every meal at restaurants in tourist areas makes your food bill snowball. But with just a little legwork, you can enjoy food that's far cheaper — and often more delicious. Street food is the budget traveler's best friend. Pad Thai in Thailand, banh mi in Vietnam, tacos in Mexico, kebabs in Turkey — each country's street food is the best way to taste the real local flavor while keeping costs down.

Seeking out local eateries is important too. Step just a block or two off the main tourist drag and you'll find great-value spots that locals frequent. Search for highly rated local restaurants on Google Maps, or ask a local for recommendations, and you'll pay local prices instead of tourist prices. Visiting a local market is also a great way to buy fresh fruit, bread, cheese, and more very cheaply — perfect for snacks or a light meal.

Choosing accommodation that includes breakfast is another good strategy. Many hostels and guesthouses offer free breakfast. A hearty breakfast means you can go light on lunch, saving even more on food. If you're staying at an Airbnb, buying groceries at the supermarket and cooking for yourself is an excellent approach.

4. Making the Most of Free Attractions and Sights

Many cities around the world have wonderful attractions you can enjoy for free. London's British Museum, National Gallery, and Tate Modern all offer free admission. Strolling inside Paris's Notre-Dame Cathedral, up Montmartre hill, or down the Champs-Élysées costs nothing either. The same goes for New York's Central Park, the Brooklyn Bridge, Times Square, and more.

Many paid museums and galleries also offer free admission on certain days or at certain times. The Louvre in Paris, for example, is free on the first Saturday evening of each month, and New York's Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) is free on Friday evenings. Check your destination's free-admission schedule before you travel and adjust your itinerary accordingly to save big on entry fees.

Take full advantage of free walking tours, too. Most European cities run free walking tours led by local guides. They operate on a tip basis, so you pay only as much as you're satisfied with at the end. These tours give you a deep understanding of a city's history and culture, delivering value on par with paid tours.

5. Relying on Public Transit

Taxis and Uber are convenient, but transport costs pile up fast. Leaning on public transit can slash your transport spending by more than 80%. Most big cities offer day passes or transit cards, which are far more economical than paying per ride. Tokyo's 24-hour Tokyo Metro pass, London's Travelcard, and Paris's Navigo pass are prime examples.

There are cheap options for intercity travel too. In Europe, budget buses (like FlixBus) or budget airlines (Ryanair, easyJet, etc.) let you get around far more cheaply than the train. In Southeast Asia, local buses or minivans can move you between cities for about a tenth of the cost of a taxi.

6. Having a Currency Strategy

Depending on how you exchange money, the amount of local currency you actually receive for the same sum can vary considerably. The most basic rule is never exchange money at the airport. Airport exchange counters are convenient but offer very unfavorable rates. You'll get a much better rate at an exchange office or bank in the city center.

Using local ATMs is another good approach. If you get a debit card with free or low overseas ATM withdrawal fees (such as Toss Bank or KakaoBank in Korea) before you go, you can withdraw only as much as you need on the ground — no worrying about leftover foreign cash. Just be sure to choose to withdraw in "local currency" when using an ATM. If you pick the option to be charged in your home currency, Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) kicks in and applies an unfavorable exchange rate.

Before your trip, plan whether you'll pay mainly by card or cash. Card payments incur a foreign transaction fee (usually 1–1.5%), but that can actually be more economical than the risk of carrying large amounts of cash. Europe and Japan in particular have excellent card acceptance, so it's efficient to exchange only a minimum of cash and put the rest on your card.

7. Preventing Unexpected Costs with Travel Insurance

Travel insurance isn't a cost — it's an investment. For a premium of around $10–20, it can cover medical bills, lost belongings, and flight-delay expenses that run from hundreds to tens of thousands of dollars. Getting medical care abroad can land you with a bill beyond anything you'd imagine. In the U.S., a single ER visit can cost thousands of dollars, and a hospital stay can run tens of thousands a day.

You can sign up for travel insurance easily online, and the premium varies with the length of your trip and your destination. For a short trip it's only around $10–20, so we recommend buying it even on a tight budget. When purchasing, check the coverage details carefully — especially the medical coverage limit and whether lost belongings are covered.

8. Taking Advantage of Duty-Free Perks

If shopping is one of your travel joys, make the most of duty-free perks. In most European countries, you can get a VAT (value-added tax) refund on purchases above a certain amount. Europe's VAT is typically 15–25%, so if you buy pricey items you can get a substantial sum back. Ask for the "Tax Free" service at the time of purchase, and complete the refund process at the airport before departure.

Shopping at the airport duty-free stores on your way out is also a good move. Cosmetics, perfume, liquor, tobacco, and the like are 20–30% cheaper at duty-free than at home. That said, unless it's something you truly need for your trip, there's no reason to overbuy. Part of the essence of budget travel is cutting out unnecessary spending.

9. Building Efficient Routes with an AI Travel Planner

An efficient route is money saved on transport. Grouping attractions in the same area into a single day cuts out needless travel, saving not just transport costs but time as well. On a trip where time is money, designing an efficient route is one of the most important money-saving strategies there is.

Because it factors in the distance between attractions, travel times, opening hours, and more to arrange your day efficiently, it's far more effective than planning by hand. In fact, analysis shows that AI-built routes involve an average of 20–30% less travel distance than manually planned ones.

An AI planner also recommends free attractions and affordable eateries, helping you build the best possible trip for your budget. It can even make fine adjustments — slotting in indoor attractions on rainy days and outdoor activities on clear ones by factoring in the weather.

The key formula for smart travel: efficient routes = less travel = lower transport costs + more sightseeing time. An AI planner makes this formula easy to put into practice.

10. Splitting Costs with Group Travel

The most direct way to lower travel costs is to travel together and split the bill. For many expenses — lodging, taxis, rental cars, food — the cost per person drops sharply as the group grows. A two-person Airbnb is often cheaper than a single hotel room, and splitting it between two people lowers the per-person cost even further.

If you don't have a friend to travel with, try using social features. myTravel's social features can connect you with people traveling to the same destination at the same time. Even just sharing part of your itinerary or swapping information can raise the quality of your trip. And with the cost-splitting feature, you can easily manage shared expenses and settle up effortlessly after the trip.

Joining day tours or group activities is another way to save. Group tours are much cheaper than private ones, and the experience of sharing them with other travelers becomes a special memory in itself. Signing up for group tours or activities organized by your hostel lets you enjoy a variety of experiences at a reasonable cost.

In Closing: Traveling Smart Means Traveling Richer

Budget travel is by no means "poor travel." On the contrary, it's a way of traveling that lets you go deeper into local culture and have genuine experiences as a traveler rather than a tourist. Eating street food and glimpsing the daily life of locals, riding public transit and discovering the true face of a city, chatting with travelers from all over the world at a hostel — these are experiences you could never have at a luxury hotel. They're the special value that only budget travel offers.

You don't need to apply all 10 methods introduced in this article. Just pick the ones that fit your situation and travel style. What matters is gathering information ahead of time and making a plan.
