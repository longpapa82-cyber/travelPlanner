Hotel vs Airbnb vs Guesthouse: Choosing Lodging by Travel Style

2026-07-25 | Hoonjae Park · myTravel Travel Content Team

Choose Lodging by 'What You Can Give Up,' Not Just Price

Most people start with price when picking a place to stay. But at the same cost, what you get and what you give up differs completely by lodging type. A cheap place in a bad location can cost more in transport, while a pricier hotel that includes breakfast, towels, and cleaning can come out ahead.

So the starting point isn't "how much" but "what do I value most on this trip." Privacy, location, meeting locals, or just a place to sleep — the right answer changes accordingly.

This guide compares four common lodging types by travel style, budget, and group size to help you choose without regrets.

Hotels: When Predictability and Convenience Come First

A hotel is the lowest-risk choice. Check-in times are clear, cleaning, towels, and amenities are provided daily, and there's a 24-hour front desk to ask. For a first-time city, a schedule with variables like a late-night arrival or pre-dawn departure, or a trip whose whole point is to rest, a hotel is safe.

The downsides: the room can be small for the price, and with no kitchen you buy every meal out. Before booking, always check whether breakfast is included, how central it is, and the cancellation policy. A free-cancellation rate is worth a little extra when your plans are fluid.

Airbnb: When You Need Space, a Kitchen, or a Local Neighborhood

Airbnb (short-term rentals) shines with larger groups or longer stays. Several people sharing a living room and splitting bedrooms often costs less per person than a hotel, and a kitchen means groceries and home cooking cut food costs sharply. It also suits people who want to stay in a residential neighborhood rather than a tourist zone.

In exchange, check-in depends on communicating with the host, and cleaning fees plus service charges can push the total above the listed price. Before booking, read the reviews carefully (especially on location, cleanliness, and host responsiveness) and confirm the exact location, check-in method, and cancellation policy.

Guesthouses and Hostels: When Budget and Meeting People Come First

Guesthouses and hostels are the cheapest option and the best place to meet other travelers. A dormitory (several people to a room) slashes the nightly cost, and common areas are great for swapping tips or finding travel companions. They suit solo backpacking, long trips, and tight budgets.

You do give up privacy and quiet, and you'll need to mind luggage storage and valuables. Check whether personal lockers are provided, whether bathrooms and showers are shared, and whether there's a curfew or quiet hours. If you're traveling alone, weigh safety too — covered further in the solo travel safety guide.

The Deciders: Location, Cancellation, Reviews

Once you've picked a type, these three matter more than price when choosing a specific place.

1) Location — a cheap place loses if daily round-trip transport and time cost more. Check on a map how close it is to your main plans and transit.
2) Cancellation policy — if plans might change, pick a free-cancellation option. A few dollars prevents a big loss.
3) Reviews — read the content of recent reviews, not just the star rating. Mentions of location, cleanliness, noise, and response time reveal the real experience best.

Once chosen, note the location and check-in time alongside your itinerary so you don't wander on arrival day. To manage lodging together with each day's plan, you can add the address and booking details as an activity in myTravel.

Wrap-Up: The Right Answer Changes by Trip

There's no absolute best. Hotel to rest easy, Airbnb for larger groups or longer stays, guesthouse when budget and meeting people come first — match the nature of the trip. And whatever the type, checking location, cancellation policy, and recent reviews avoids the big failures.
