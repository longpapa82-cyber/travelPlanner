Istanbul 4-Day First-Trip Itinerary: A Complete Route Through Sultanahmet, the Grand Bazaar, and the Bosphorus

2026-07-30 | myTravel Team

Your First Istanbul: Why 4 Days Is Just Right

Istanbul is a city that straddles two continents, Europe and Asia, and layer upon layer of Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman history is stacked here. Within a single city you'll find world-class monuments like Hagia Sophia, a lively market like the Grand Bazaar, and the scenery of the Bosphorus strait, so for a first visit it's easy to feel lost about where to begin.

Four days is the ideal length to comfortably sample the heart of Istanbul's old city, its new district, and both continents. The most stable structure is to spend the first day on travel and getting oriented in the Sultanahmet area, focus the second and third days on the markets and a strait cruise, and on the last day finish a morning itinerary before heading to the airport. Since the monument-dense Sultanahmet is mostly connected on foot, the key is to group the areas together as you go.

In this article, we've organized things around a day-by-day route grouped by area so that first-time Istanbul travelers can follow it as is. Travelers from many countries can stay visa-free for up to 90 days, so the preparation burden tends to be light as well.

Day 1: Airport Arrival and the Sultanahmet Monuments

It depends on your arrival time, but assuming you land in the afternoon, it's best to spend the first day going through the old city's iconic buildings in the order of hotel check-in → Hagia Sophia → Blue Mosque.

From the Airport to the City

  • Istanbul Airport (IST): It's some distance from the city, so it takes about an hour to reach the center by Havaist airport bus or the metro (M11). If you have a lot of luggage the bus is convenient, and if you want to avoid traffic the metro is handy.
  • İstanbulkart: A rechargeable transit card that lets you use trams, buses, ferries, and the subway with a single card. Buying one in advance at the airport makes getting around afterward much smoother.

Hagia Sophia

With nearly 1,500 years of history, Hagia Sophia went from cathedral to mosque, then through a period as a museum, and is currently operated as a mosque. Admission is free, but entry is restricted during Islamic prayer times, and inside you need clothing that covers your shoulders and knees. Women may find it helpful to bring a scarf.

Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque)

The Blue Mosque, directly across from Hagia Sophia, is famous for its six minarets and blue tile decoration. This is also free admission; visit outside prayer times and remove your shoes before entering. Since the two buildings face each other, they're perfect to explore together on your first afternoon.

Day 2: Topkapi Palace and the Grand Bazaar & Spice Market

On the second day you'll group the Ottoman palace and Istanbul's two signature markets into one axis and walk it. All three are within the old city, so you can cover them fully on foot and by tram.

Morning: Topkapi Palace

The palace where the Ottoman sultans lived for nearly 400 years, its highlights are the treasury, the Harem, and the garden overlooking the Bosphorus strait. It's paid admission, and the Harem has a separate fee. It's large, so it's best to set aside two or three hours.

Afternoon: Grand Bazaar

One of the world's oldest covered markets, with more than 4,000 shops connected like a maze. As you walk around looking at carpets, lamps, ceramics, and gold and silver work, you'll lose track of time. Haggling is the basic culture here, so try negotiating generously down from the first price quoted. It's a crowded place, so watch out for pickpockets.

Evening: Spice Market (Egyptian Bazaar)

Within walking distance of the Grand Bazaar, the Spice Market is filled with colorful spices, Turkish delight (lokum), nuts, and tea. It's great for buying souvenirs and gifts, and it's a nice spot to wrap up the day with a mackerel sandwich (balık ekmek) in the evening at the nearby Eminönü pier.

Day 3: A Bosphorus Cruise, Galata, and Two Continents

On the third day, centered on the Bosphorus strait, Istanbul's true charm, you'll take in both the European and Asian sides together. The fact that a single ferry can carry you between two continents is what makes this city special.

Morning: Basilica Cistern

Near Sultanahmet, the underground cistern is a huge water reservoir built in the Byzantine era. The dark interior lined with 336 columns and the upside-down Medusa head columns create a mysterious atmosphere. It's not large, so an hour is enough to look around fully.

Afternoon: Bosphorus Cruise

Take a ferry departing from Eminönü pier and you can admire the palaces, fortresses, and grand mansions lining the strait. You can enjoy it cheaply even on a regular city ferry, and if you wish, cross over to the Asian side (Üsküdar / Kadıköy) to feel a local atmosphere different from Europe. A single İstanbulkart covers the ferry too.

Evening: Galata Tower and İstiklal Street

Climb Galata Tower in the new district and the old city's skyline and the strait come into view at a glance. It's especially beautiful around sunset. Afterward, walk along İstiklal Street, taking in the red vintage tram, shops, and cafes, to fully enjoy Istanbul's lively night.

Day 4: A Morning Stroll and Departure

On the last day, wrap things up with a morning stroll and shopping before heading to the airport, timed to your flight. Avoid traveling far with heavy luggage, and plan your final itinerary somewhere close to your hotel or the airport route.

Recommended Way to Wrap Up

  • Gülhane Park: A quiet park next to Topkapi Palace, perfect for a relaxed start with a morning stroll and a cup of tea.
  • Last souvenir shopping: Lokum, Turkish tea, and spices are small in bulk, so they make good last gifts. It's convenient to buy them all at once at the Spice Market or a large supermarket.
  • A cup of Turkish çay: The black tea çay, served in a tulip-shaped glass, is a symbol of Istanbul. Sit at a cafe and enjoy the last lingering moments.

Airport Arrival Time

For international flights, it's safe to arrive at the airport 2-3 hours before departure. Because Istanbul Airport is far from the city, you need to calculate generously, including the travel time for the Havaist bus or metro. Traffic is heavy during commute hours, so leave yourself plenty of margin.

Istanbul Transport and Budget Guide

What first-time Istanbul travelers worry about most is transport and currency exchange. Public transit is simpler than you'd think—a single İstanbulkart handles trams, buses, ferries, and the subway all at once—and you can check routes and fares with the Google Maps app.

Transport Tips

  • İstanbulkart: A rechargeable transit card that works across trams, buses, ferries, and the subway, making it the most convenient. It also applies a transfer discount between transit modes.
  • Tram (T1): Most of the Sultanahmet monuments lie along the T1 tram line, so it's especially useful for getting around the old city.
  • Ferry: Besides crossing Europe ↔ Asia, it can also be used as a cheap way to sightsee the Bosphorus.

Currency Exchange and Safety

The currency is the Turkish lira (TRY), and it tends to have large exchange-rate swings. In-city exchange offices often have better rates than the airport, so it's advantageous to change only a small amount at the airport and exchange more as needed. Be especially careful of pickpockets in the crowds at the Grand Bazaar, on trams, and at tourist sites.

Rough 4-Day Budget (per person)

  • Airfare: Varies widely by season and whether the route has layovers
  • Accommodation: From guesthouses to boutique hotels for 3 nights
  • Food: Local prices of roughly USD 25-50 per day
  • Transport: Roughly USD 5-10 per day (based on İstanbulkart top-ups)
  • Admissions & shopping: Including paid monuments like Topkapi Palace and souvenirs

Because the perceived cost changes greatly with the exchange rate, it's best to check the lira rate before departure and set your budget accordingly.
