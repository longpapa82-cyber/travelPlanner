Bangkok in 4 Nights: Temples, Night Markets, and Rooftop Bars

2026-07-10 | Hoonjae Park · myTravel Travel Content Team

Why Bangkok Works as a 4-Night Trip

Bangkok packs a lot into a compact core, which is exactly why four nights and five days hits the sweet spot. You get enough time for the grand temples along the Chao Phraya River, a floating or weekend market, at least one proper rooftop sunset, and a day trip out of the city without feeling like you're sprinting from stop to stop.

The trick is grouping sights by neighborhood so you're not crossing the city twice a day. Bangkok traffic is legendary, and a 6 km taxi ride at rush hour can easily take 45 minutes. This itinerary clusters each day around a single area and leans on the BTS Skytrain, the MRT subway, and river boats to skip the gridlock.

Below is a realistic route for a first visit, with actual travel times, ballpark costs in Thai baht (THB), and honest notes on what to skip when you're short on time.

Day 1: Arrival and Riverside Warm-Up

Most long-haul flights land at Suvarnabhumi (BKK) in the afternoon or evening. From the airport, the Airport Rail Link runs to Phaya Thai in about 30 minutes for 45 THB, where you connect to the BTS. A metered taxi to the city center costs roughly 300 to 450 THB including tolls and takes 45 to 60 minutes depending on traffic.

Keep the first day light. Check in, then head to the riverside near ICONSIAM, a mall on the Chao Phraya with a free shuttle boat from Sathorn Pier. The ground-floor food hall, SookSiam, recreates street stalls from all over Thailand, so it's an easy, air-conditioned introduction to Thai food after a long flight.

Evening: First Rooftop

If you still have energy, ease into Bangkok's rooftop scene somewhere relaxed rather than the big-name spots. Many sky bars enforce a smart-casual dress code (no shorts or open sandals for men), so pack one collared shirt and closed shoes. Expect cocktails from around 350 to 550 THB.

Day 2: The Grand Temples

This is the classic Bangkok day, so start early. The temples get crowded and brutally hot by late morning, and dress codes are strictly enforced: shoulders and knees covered for everyone.

  1. Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew (opens 8:30 a.m.): The former royal residence and the Temple of the Emerald Buddha. Entry is 500 THB. Budget about two hours.
  2. Wat Pho (a 10-minute walk south): Home to the 46-meter Reclining Buddha. Entry is 300 THB, which includes a bottle of water. This is also the home of traditional Thai massage; a 30-minute foot massage here runs about 280 THB.
  3. Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn): Cross the river on the ferry from Tha Tien Pier for 5 THB. The porcelain-covered spire is stunning up close, and it photographs beautifully from the opposite bank at sunset.

Getting to the temple district is easiest by river: take the BTS to Saphan Taksin, then the Chao Phraya Express Boat (orange flag, 16 THB) to Tha Chang Pier.

Watch the Scams

Near the Grand Palace, be wary of friendly strangers who insist the temple is "closed today" and offer a tuk-tuk tour instead. It's a well-worn scam. The palace is open; walk to the official entrance and check for yourself.

Day 3: Markets, Day and Night

Bangkok's markets deserve a full day, and how you plan it depends on whether your trip includes a weekend.

If It's a Weekend: Chatuchak

The Chatuchak Weekend Market (Saturday and Sunday only) has more than 15,000 stalls across 27 sections, from vintage clothing to plants to street food. Take the MRT to Kamphaeng Phet or the BTS to Mo Chit. Go early, around 9 to 10 a.m., before the aisles turn into a crush. Bring cash, wear light clothing, and set a meeting point in case your group splits up.

If It's a Weekday: Floating Market Trip

On weekdays, consider a half-day trip to Damnoen Saduak or the more local Amphawa floating market. These sit 80 to 110 km from the city, so a driver or organized tour (around 1,200 to 1,800 THB per person) makes the logistics far simpler than public transport.

Evening: Night Market

Wind down at a night market such as Jodd Fairs near Rama IX (MRT Phra Ram 9). It's open-air, full of local food stalls, and famous for its giant "leng saap" pork rib bowls. Most dishes run 60 to 150 THB, and it stays lively until late.

Day 4: Rooftops, Shopping, and a Slower Pace

By day four you've earned a slower morning. Spend it in the Siam and Sukhumvit area, which is where Bangkok's malls, cafes, and skytrain lines converge.

  • Jim Thompson House: A tranquil teak compound turned museum, dedicated to the American who revived Thai silk. Entry is 200 THB, and it's a genuinely cool, green escape from the heat.
  • Siam Paragon and MBK Center: Two very different malls a short walk apart, one glossy and luxury-focused, the other a warren of phone accessories and bargains.
  • Lumpini Park: If you want green space instead of shopping, this central park has monitor lizards, pedal boats, and free outdoor aerobics classes at dusk.

The Sunset You Came For

Save your best rooftop for tonight. Options range from the famous perch above the Lebua hotel to lower-key spots in Thonglor. Arrive 30 to 40 minutes before sunset (around 6:30 p.m. most of the year) to claim a good spot, and check the dress code before you go. A round of cocktails for two typically lands between 900 and 1,400 THB.

Day 5: One Last Bite and Departure

Your fifth day usually revolves around your flight time. If you're leaving in the afternoon or evening, squeeze in a final breakfast of jok (rice porridge) or a mango sticky rice run before checking out.

Leave a generous buffer for the airport. Aim to arrive at Suvarnabhumi three hours before an international departure, and remember that afternoon traffic can add 30 minutes or more to the taxi ride. The Airport Rail Link is the reliable, traffic-proof backup if the roads are jammed.

Rough Budget Snapshot

For a mid-range trip, here's a realistic per-person daily estimate, excluding flights and accommodation:

  • Food: 500 to 900 THB (mixing street stalls and a couple of sit-down meals)
  • Transport: 150 to 300 THB (BTS, MRT, boats, and short taxi hops)
  • Attractions: 300 to 800 THB on temple days, less on others
  • Rooftop or night out: 700 to 1,400 THB when you go

Weather, Timing, and Practical Notes

Bangkok is hot year-round, but the season shapes your plans more than most cities.

  • Cool season (November to February): The best window. Daytime highs around 32°C, low humidity, minimal rain. It's also peak tourist season, so book temples and rooftops with that in mind.
  • Hot season (March to May): Punishing heat, often 38°C or more. Front-load outdoor sightseeing to the morning and retreat to malls or a spa in the afternoon.
  • Rainy season (June to October): Short, heavy downpours, usually in the late afternoon. Carry a compact umbrella and keep an indoor backup plan for each day. Flooding on some streets can snarl traffic fast.

A few more essentials: get a local SIM or eSIM at the airport, carry small bills for street food and boats, and download a ride-hailing app as a fair-price alternative to haggling with tuk-tuks. Tap water isn't drinkable, but bottled water is cheap everywhere.
