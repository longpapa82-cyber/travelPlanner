Barcelona 4-Day Itinerary: Key Routes from Gaudí to the Gothic Quarter and the Beach

2026-07-30 | myTravel Team

First Time in Barcelona: Why 4 Days Is Just Right

Barcelona, the heart of Spain's Catalonia region, is a rare destination where Gaudí's architecture, medieval alleys, and Mediterranean beaches all come together in a single city. But once you start listing everywhere you want to go—the Sagrada Família, Park Güell, the Gothic Quarter, Las Ramblas, Barceloneta Beach, and Montjuïc—it can feel overwhelming to know where to begin.

Four days is the ideal length for comfortably covering the essentials of Barcelona. On the first day, you walk through the city center (the Gothic Quarter and Las Ramblas) to get a feel for the city; on the second day, you take in Gaudí's architecture all at once; on the third day, you relax at the beach and up on Montjuïc hill; and on the last day, you fit in a morning schedule before heading to the airport. This is a stable structure. The key is to group your visits by area.

In this article, we've organized everything around a daily route grouped by area so that first-time Barcelona travelers can follow it as-is. Korean nationals can stay in Spain visa-free for up to 90 days, so no separate visa preparation is needed.

Day 1: Arrival at the Airport and the Gothic Quarter and Las Ramblas

It depends on your arrival time, but assuming you land in the afternoon, it's good to spend the first day lightly walking through the city center in this order: hotel check-in → Gothic Quarter → Las Ramblas and the Boqueria Market.

From the Airport to the City

  • Aerobús (airport bus): About 30–40 minutes from El Prat Airport to Plaça de Catalunya. Since it takes you straight to the city center, it's convenient when you have a lot of luggage.
  • Metro L9 Sud: A metro line connecting the airport and the city; you can transfer to reach downtown. A separate fare applies to the airport section, so be sure to check.

The Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic)

The Gothic Quarter, a tangle of Roman-era walls and medieval alleys, is the best area to explore on foot. Take your time walking through the Barcelona Cathedral, Plaça del Rei, and the small squares tucked between narrow stone streets to meet the old face of the city. The lanes are narrow and winding, which actually makes walking them all the more fun.

Las Ramblas and the Boqueria Market

Las Ramblas, which continues from the Gothic Quarter, is Barcelona's signature promenade stretching from Plaça de Catalunya to the harbor. At the Boqueria Market (Mercat de la Boqueria) along the way, it's a great spot to enjoy jamón, fresh fruit juice, and tapas for a light first-evening meal.

myTravel Tip The area around Las Ramblas and the Boqueria Market is popular with tourists, which also makes it a frequent spot for pickpockets. Wear your bag in front and keep an eye on your phone and wallet. Day 2: Gaudí's Barcelona

On the second day, focus on Gaudí's architecture, which made Barcelona a world-famous city. You'll group the Sagrada Família, Casa Batlló, and Park Güell into a single circuit, and since all three are highly popular, online advance booking is practically essential.

Morning: Sagrada Família

Gaudí's unfinished basilica is the symbol of a Barcelona trip. The morning hours, when light streaming through the stained glass colors the interior, are especially beautiful. On-site ticket lines are long and tend to sell out, so it's best to book online in advance for a set date and time. Tickets that include tower views are configured separately.

Afternoon: Casa Batlló

Casa Batlló, on Passeig de Gràcia, is one of Gaudí's residential works, alive with curves and color. If you visit the nearby Casa Milà (La Pedrera) as well, you can compare Gaudí's residential architecture along a single street. This area is also a shopping street lined with luxury boutiques and cafés.

Evening: Park Güell

This hilltop park is famous for its colorful mosaic tiles and undulating benches. The paid entry area (the Monumental Zone) operates on a timed reservation system, so book your time in advance. The view of Barcelona from the hill is especially lovely around sunset.

  • Route tip: Sagrada Família → Casa Batlló is a few metro stops, and Casa Batlló → Park Güell is by bus or metro. If you space out the reservation times for the three sites across morning, afternoon, and evening, you won't feel rushed while moving between them.

Day 3: Barceloneta Beach and Montjuïc

The third day is for enjoying the leisurely Mediterranean pace. Spend the morning at Barceloneta Beach, and in the afternoon head up Montjuïc hill to take in both the views and the cultural spaces.

Morning: Barceloneta Beach

This is an urban beach close to downtown by metro. Walk along the beachfront promenade or rest for a while on the sand and soak in the Mediterranean atmosphere. Restaurants serving seafood line the beachfront, making it a great place to try paella or fresh seafood tapas for lunch.

Afternoon: Montjuïc Hill

On Montjuïc hill in the southwest of Barcelona, you'll find Montjuïc Castle, the National Art Museum of Catalonia (MNAC), the Olympic Stadium, and the Magic Fountain, all gathered together. You can go up the hill by cable car or funicular (an incline railway), so the journey itself becomes a sight to see. Don't miss the city view from the steps in front of MNAC either.

Evening: Spanish Dining Culture

In Spain, dinner tends to be late, so many restaurants open after 8 or 9 p.m. Some shops also close for a siesta during the day, so it's natural to focus on tourist sites in the early afternoon and schedule meals a bit later, in tune with the local rhythm.

Day 4: A Morning Stroll and Departure

On the last day, wrap things up with a morning stroll and shopping, then head to the airport according to your flight time. Avoid traveling far with heavy luggage, and it's best to plan your final schedule somewhere close to your accommodation or the airport route.

Recommended Way to Wrap Up

  • The Gothic Quarter and El Born alleys: A great place to walk again through the alleys you didn't fully see on the first day and browse souvenir shops and small cafés.
  • Around Plaça de Catalunya: With large department stores and shops gathered here, it's a good place to finish all your gift shopping at once.
  • Relaxing at a café: Enjoy a leisurely final morning with a Catalan-style breakfast (croissant and coffee).

Airport Arrival Time

For international flights, arriving at the airport 2 to 3 hours before departure is safe. Tax refunds, checking luggage, and security screening take more time than you might expect, so leave plenty of margin. From the city to El Prat Airport, it's best to calculate generous travel time by Aerobús or metro.

Barcelona Transport and Budget Guide

Barcelona's city center is well served by the Metro and buses, so getting around isn't difficult. When you're confused about routes, just enter your origin and destination in the Google Maps app and it will guide you through transfers and fares, so there's no need to worry much.

Transit Cards

  • T-casual: A 10-ride pass, a good basic card for sharing rides across the metro, bus, and tram during a short trip. Most travel is covered within Zone 1 (the city center).
  • Hop-on ticket (tourist transit pass): If your itinerary involves taking public transport many times over a set period, a period pass is also an option, with unlimited rides during the valid period. Estimate your number of trips and compare it against the T-casual before choosing.

Rough 4-Day Budget (per person)

  • Airfare: 800,000–1,500,000 KRW depending on the season and whether there are layovers
  • Accommodation: 250,000–600,000 KRW for 3 nights (hostel to mid-range hotel)
  • Food: 30–60 euros per day
  • Transport: About 1–2 T-casual passes over 4 days
  • Admission and shopping: Gaudí site admissions are included, so it varies a lot by person, but budget a comfortable 100,000–150,000 KRW

Barcelona's currency is the euro (EUR), and since the perceived cost changes with the exchange rate, it's best to check the euro exchange rate before departure and set your budget accordingly.
