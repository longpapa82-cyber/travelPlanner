First Dubai Trip 3 Nights 4 Days Itinerary: A Complete Route for Burj Khalifa, Old Dubai, and Desert Safari

2026-07-30 | myTravel Team

Your First Dubai Trip: Why 3 Nights 4 Days Is Just Right

Dubai is about 9 to 10 hours by direct flight from Korea, and it's a unique destination where the world's tallest building, an endless desert, and old souk markets all coexist in a single city. But when you actually sit down to plan, Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall, the desert safari, Palm Jumeirah, and Old Dubai are all completely different in character and spread far apart, which can leave you feeling overwhelmed.

3 nights 4 days is the ideal length for comfortably touring Dubai's different faces. Spend one day in the ultra-modern Downtown, one day soaking up the atmosphere of old Dubai, and one day across the desert and beach resorts, and you can fully feel the city's contrasts. Since the sights are far apart by area, the key is to group places with a similar character together on the same day.

In this article, we've organized everything around daily routes grouped by area so that first-time Dubai travelers can follow along directly. For the best time to visit, we strongly recommend November through March, when you can avoid the intense heat. In midsummer, daytime temperatures exceed 45 degrees Celsius, making outdoor activities practically difficult.

Day 1: Airport Arrival and Downtown (Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall, Fountain Show)

Dubai International Airport (DXB) is close to the city center, so it's great to enjoy the downtown area from your very first day. It depends on your arrival time, but assuming you land in the afternoon, on the first day it's best to tour Downtown in the order of hotel check-in → Dubai Mall → Burj Khalifa → fountain show.

From the Airport to the City

  • Metro Red Line: From DXB Airport (Terminals 1 and 3), the Metro Red Line connects directly toward Downtown and the Marina. Fares vary by zone, and if you top up a Nol card before boarding, you can use the metro, buses, and trams all with a single card.
  • Taxi: If you have a lot of luggage or arrive at night, an airport taxi is convenient. Taxis run on a meter and reach the city center without much trouble.

Dubai Mall

Dubai Mall, one of the world's largest shopping malls, is a space like a city of its own, gathering not just shopping but also an indoor aquarium, a huge fountain, and hundreds of restaurants. The entrance to the Burj Khalifa observation deck is also inside the mall, so it flows naturally into your next stop.

Burj Khalifa Observation Deck

At 828 meters tall, it's the tallest building in the world. From the observation decks on the 124th and 125th floors or higher, you can look down over the whole of Dubai, and the sunset time slot is especially popular, so booking in advance is recommended. When buying on-site, your preferred time slot is often sold out.

The Dubai Fountain Show

The fountain show held every evening on the lake between Dubai Mall and Burj Khalifa is the best sight you can watch for free. Wrap up your first day watching the jets of water rise in time with the music from the mall's outdoor terrace or the lakeside promenade.

Day 2: Old Dubai (Deira, Gold Souk, Abra Water Taxi)

On the second day, walk through old Dubai, which contrasts with the ultra-modern Downtown. Deira and Bur Dubai face each other across Dubai Creek (the canal), so the classic way to tour is to cross back and forth on a traditional ferry, the abra.

Morning: Gold Souk and Spice Souk

Deira's Gold Souk is a traditional market where gold jewelry lines the walls, and it's a treat for the eyes just to browse. In the Spice Souk right next door, the scent of saffron, spices, nuts, and dates fills the alleys. Bargaining is routine here, so ask about prices and compare as you look around.

Midday: Abra Water Taxi

The abra, a traditional wooden boat that crosses Dubai Creek, is very cheap at around 1 dirham. The short boat ride from Deira to Bur Dubai, with the river breeze on your face, is an experience that feels like a symbol of an old Dubai trip.

Afternoon: Al Fahidi Historical District

On the Bur Dubai side is the Al Fahidi Historical District, where narrow alleys and traditional wind-tower (barjeel) architecture remain. The Dubai Museum, charming cafes, and galleries are gathered here, making it a good place to quietly take in the city's old appearance.

Day 3: Desert Safari and Palm Jumeirah, Marina

The third day is for enjoying Dubai's nature and resorts. Touring the city at a relaxed pace during the day and heading to the desert safari in the late afternoon is the best arrangement, since it avoids the intense heat while letting you capture the desert sunset.

Midday: Palm Jumeirah

Palm Jumeirah, an artificial island built in the shape of a palm tree by reclaiming the sea, is one of Dubai's signature landmarks. The large resorts, water park, and viewpoints at the tip of the island are famous, and you can ride the monorail across the island to enjoy the scenery.

Midday: Dubai Marina

The Marina, where high-rise buildings surround a canal, is a stylish area lined with promenades, yachts, and cafes. JBR Beach is nearby, so it's great to walk along the seaside after lunch, and it's also counted among the best spots for evening night views.

Afternoon to Evening: Desert Safari

The desert safari is the highlight of a Dubai trip. In most cases, a local tour operator picks you up at your hotel and takes you to the desert, then bundles together 4WD dune bashing (driving over sand dunes), a camel experience, and dinner and a show at a desert camp. Afternoon-departure tours are popular because you can capture the red light of the desert at sunset.

Day 4: A Relaxed Morning and Departure

On the last day, wrap up with a relaxed morning schedule followed by heading to the airport, timed to your flight. Avoid traveling far with heavy luggage, and it's best to spend your final hours somewhere close to your hotel or airport route.

Recommended Wrap-Up Route

  • Revisit Dubai Mall: It's a good place to buy stores, souvenirs, or dates and spices you didn't get to on the first day. It's indoors, so no worries about the heat.
  • Mall of the Emirates: A large mall famous for its indoor ski slope; if your plans are on the opposite side from the airport, it's a good spot for a quick look around.
  • Hotel Lounge or Pool: If a late checkout is possible, spending your final morning relaxing at the hotel is also a good choice.

Airport Arrival Time

For international flights, arriving at the airport 3 hours before departure is safe. DXB is very large and security screening and processing take time, so give yourself some margin. If you're traveling by metro, check the operating hours and the bulk of your luggage in advance, and if you're departing in the early morning or late at night, taking a taxi is the safer option.

Dubai Transport, Budget, and Local Rules Guide

We've organized the transport, budget, and local rules that first-time Dubai travelers wonder about. Because the sights are spread far apart, your travel plan greatly influences how satisfying your trip is.

Transit Passes

  • Nol card: A rechargeable transit card that works on the metro, buses, trams, and even the abra, making it the most convenient. For a first Dubai trip, this single card is enough for public transport.
  • Metro Red Line: The core line connecting the airport, Downtown, and the Marina. It's well air-conditioned, so it's also good for escaping the heat.
  • Taxis and ride-hailing: For places the metro doesn't reach, like the desert or Palm Jumeirah, taxis or ride-hailing apps are convenient.

Rough Budget for 3 Nights 4 Days (Per Person)

  • Airfare: 700,000 to 1,200,000 won depending on the season
  • Accommodation: 300,000 to 900,000 won for 3 nights (wide range from mid-range hotels to luxury resorts)
  • Food: 100 to 250 dirhams per day
  • Transport: 20 to 40 dirhams per day (higher when using taxis)
  • Activities like Burj Khalifa and the desert safari: Varies a lot by person, but budget a comfortable 150,000 to 300,000 won

Local Rules Worth Knowing

  • Weekend: The UAE weekend is Saturday and Sunday. Operating hours of government offices and some facilities may differ from weekdays.
  • Dress: In malls and on the street, neat attire covering the shoulders and knees is a safe choice. When visiting mosques, especially conservative attire is required.
  • Alcohol: Alcohol is only allowed in designated places such as hotels and licensed restaurants, and drinking in public spaces is regulated.
  • Visa and Currency: Korean citizens can stay up to 90 days visa-free. The currency used is the dirham (AED).

Since the cost you feel changes with the exchange rate, it's a good idea to check the dirham exchange rate before departure and set your budget accordingly.
