Best Destinations by Season: A Traveler's Guide to Spring, Summer, Fall, and Winter

2026-07-10 | myTravel Team

Why Timing Matters More Than the Destination

Ask ten seasoned travelers about their worst trip, and a surprising number will point to timing rather than the place itself. Kyoto in the wrong week means fighting crowds for a single photo of a temple gate. The Greek islands in mid-August mean paying peak prices for a beach you can barely find a towel-space on. The same destination can feel like a discovery in one month and a tourist trap in another.

Getting the season right does three things at once. It lands you in the best weather window, it often lowers your costs during shoulder months, and it lets you experience the thing a place is genuinely famous for, whether that is a cherry blossom in full bloom or a mountain pass blanketed in snow. A well-timed trip is the difference between checking a box and actually being there for the moment.

This guide walks through each of the four seasons with specific destinations, real bloom and weather windows, and honest notes on when to book. Rather than a generic list, think of it as a calendar you can plan around. Spring: Blossoms, Mild Weather, and Fewer Crowds

Spring is arguably the most forgiving season for a first-time international trip. Temperatures sit in a comfortable range, gardens and parks come alive, and in many regions you catch the last weeks before summer crowds arrive.

Cherry blossom cities

  • Kyoto and Tokyo, Japan — Peak bloom typically falls between late March and early April, though it shifts a week or two depending on the year. Kyoto's Philosopher's Path and Tokyo's Meguro River are classic viewing spots. Book accommodation two to three months ahead; this is the single most competitive window of the Japanese travel year.
  • Washington, D.C., USA — The Tidal Basin cherry trees usually peak in late March or early April, timed with the National Cherry Blossom Festival. Weekday mornings are far quieter than weekends.
  • Bonn, Germany — The Heerstrasse cherry tunnel in the old town blooms for a short window in mid-April and is a quieter European alternative.

Mild-weather escapes

  • Andalusia, Spain — April and May bring warm days without the brutal summer heat, ideal for Seville, Granada, and Córdoba.
  • The Netherlands — Keukenhof gardens open roughly mid-March to mid-May, with tulip fields peaking around mid-to-late April.

Summer: Coastlines, Long Days, and Northern Escapes

Summer is the default vacation season for a reason: long daylight hours, warm water, and school holidays that make it easy to travel with family. The trade-off is crowds and higher prices, so the smart move is either to embrace the peak with early planning or head somewhere that is genuinely at its best in these months.

Beaches and coastlines

  • Amalfi Coast, Italy — June and September bracket the peak and offer warm seas with slightly thinner crowds than the August rush. Positano and Ravello are the headline stops.
  • Croatia's Dalmatian Coast — Split and Dubrovnik shine from June through September. Consider basing yourself on a quieter island like Hvar or Vis to escape the cruise-ship day crowds.
  • Bali, Indonesia — The dry season runs roughly May to September, making mid-year the reliable window for beaches and rice terraces.

Cool-climate and midnight-sun escapes

  • Iceland — June and July bring nearly endless daylight, open highland roads, and accessible waterfalls. This is peak season, so book vehicles and lodging well ahead.
  • Norwegian fjords — Bergen and the fjord region are at their greenest and most navigable in summer, with long evenings for cruising.
  • Canadian Rockies — Banff and Lake Louise turn their famous turquoise in the warmer months once the meltwater fills the lakes.

Fall: Foliage, Harvests, and the Shoulder-Season Sweet Spot

For many experienced travelers, fall is the secret best season. Summer crowds thin out, prices soften, the weather stays pleasant in most regions, and the landscape puts on a show as leaves turn. It is the classic shoulder-season sweet spot.

Foliage destinations

  • New England, USA — Peak color rolls from north to south, typically hitting Vermont and New Hampshire in early-to-mid October and southern areas a bit later. Scenic drives like the Kancamagus Highway are the main event.
  • Kyoto, Japan — The autumn colors (koyo) usually peak from mid-to-late November. Temples like Tofuku-ji and the Arashiyama district are stunning, and the season is nearly as popular as spring, so plan ahead.
  • Bavaria and the Alps — Late September through October brings golden larch forests and clear hiking days before the first snow.

Harvest and festival travel

  • Tuscany, Italy — Grape and olive harvest season through September and October means wine-country tours at their liveliest.
  • Munich, Germany — Oktoberfest actually runs mostly in late September; book lodging months in advance if this is your goal.

Winter: Snow, Festivals, and Warm-Weather Reprieves

Winter splits travelers into two camps: those chasing snow and festive atmosphere, and those escaping the cold entirely. Both are valid, and both reward planning around specific dates because winter is packed with time-limited events.

Snow and festival destinations

  • Sapporo, Japan — The Sapporo Snow Festival takes place in early-to-mid February, with enormous snow and ice sculptures. Hokkaido also offers some of the world's most reliable powder skiing.
  • Central European Christmas markets — Vienna, Prague, and Strasbourg run markets from late November through December. Weekday evenings are far more relaxed than weekends.
  • Lapland, Finland — December through March is prime time for northern lights, husky sledding, and the classic Arctic winter experience.
  • Quebec City, Canada — The Winter Carnival in late January and February turns the old town into a snow-lit festival.

Warm-weather reprieves

  • Thailand — The northern hemisphere winter is Thailand's dry season, making December to February ideal for beaches and cities alike.
  • Southern hemisphere summer — Australia, New Zealand, and Patagonia flip the calendar, so December through February is their warm high season.
  • Canary Islands, Spain — Mild year-round temperatures make them a reliable European winter-sun option.

How to Choose Your Season and Book at the Right Time

Once you have a shortlist, a few simple rules of thumb help you narrow down the exact window.

  1. Chase shoulder seasons when you can. The weeks just before and after peak, such as late spring and early fall in Europe, usually deliver good weather with lower prices and fewer crowds.
  2. Book event-driven trips earliest. Cherry blossoms, major festivals, and peak foliage all draw predictable surges. Aim to lock in flights and lodging two to three months ahead for these.
  3. Match the destination to your tolerance. If heat wears you down, skip Mediterranean beaches in August and choose June or September instead. If you love empty trails, fall and early spring are your friends.
  4. Check the actual dates, not just the season. "Spring in Japan" is meaningless if you miss the two-week bloom window. Verify the specific dates before committing.
