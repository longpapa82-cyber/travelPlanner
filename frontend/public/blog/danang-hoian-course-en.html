Da Nang and Hoi An in 4 Days: Balancing Beach Time and Old Town Sightseeing

2026-07-10 | myTravel Team

Why Da Nang and Hoi An Work So Well Together

Central Vietnam is one of those rare destinations where you don't have to choose between doing nothing on a beach and filling your days with culture. Da Nang gives you a long stretch of soft-sand coastline, big resort pools, and a modern city grid, while Hoi An sits just 30 kilometers south with its lantern-lit Old Town, tailor shops, and riverside cafes. The two are close enough that you can base yourself in one and day-trip to the other.

Over three nights and four days, you can genuinely relax for half the trip and still see the highlights. The plan below assumes you land at Da Nang International Airport, which handles most direct flights from East Asia, and it's built so that the pace never feels rushed. Beach mornings, sightseeing afternoons, and long lantern-lit evenings.

Before you lock in dates, a quick note on weather: February to May is the sweet spot, with dry days and water warm enough to swim. September through December brings heavier rain and the occasional storm, so if you're chasing beach time, aim for spring. Once you know your window, the rest is just sequencing.

Day 1: Arrival and My Khe Beach

Most flights land in the late morning or early afternoon, so treat day one as a soft start. From the airport, it's only about 15 minutes by taxi or ride-hailing app to the My Khe Beach area, where most beachfront hotels cluster. Check in, drop your bags, and let the trip begin at a slow speed.

My Khe stretches for several kilometers, so you rarely feel crowded even in high season. Spend the first afternoon easing in:

  • A swim and a couple of hours on a sun lounger to shake off the flight
  • A walk along the beachfront promenade toward the seafood restaurants at the north end
  • A first dinner of grilled seafood or a bowl of mi Quang, the turmeric-tinted noodle dish that central Vietnam is known for

If you still have energy after dark, the Dragon Bridge is a short ride away and breathes fire and water on weekend nights (usually around 9 PM on Saturday and Sunday). It's touristy, but it's a fun way to end a travel day without committing to anything demanding.

Day 2: A Full Day in Hoi An Old Town

Day two is your big sightseeing day. Head to Hoi An in the late morning so you arrive as the Old Town wakes up. A private car runs roughly 400,000 to 500,000 VND each way, while a ride-hailing app is often cheaper; the drive takes about 45 minutes. Consider having the driver drop you near the Japanese Covered Bridge, the symbolic entrance to the historic quarter.

Afternoon in the Old Town

Hoi An's UNESCO-listed center is compact and best explored on foot. A single admission ticket covers entry to a set of heritage houses and assembly halls, and you'll want a few of these on your list:

  • Japanese Covered Bridge — the 400-year-old landmark, small but photogenic
  • Tan Ky Old House — a merchant home that shows how trade shaped the town
  • Fujian Assembly Hall — vivid temple architecture and quiet courtyards
  • The riverside market lanes — for a bowl of cao lau, Hoi An's signature pork-and-noodle dish

If you've been thinking about a tailored shirt or dress, Hoi An is famous for its tailors, and a same-day or next-day fitting is realistic if you order by early afternoon.

Evening lanterns

Stay until dusk. This is when the town earns its reputation. Silk lanterns switch on across the streets and along the Thu Bon River, and you can float a paper lantern from a small boat for a modest fee. It's unapologetically romantic, and it photographs beautifully. Grab a late dinner at a riverside spot before heading back to Da Nang, or consider spending one night in Hoi An if lanterns are your priority (more on that below).

Day 3: Marble Mountains and a Beach Afternoon

After a big day out, day three splits the difference between light sightseeing and pure downtime. Start in the morning while it's still cool with the Marble Mountains (Ngu Hanh Son), a cluster of five limestone-and-marble hills riddled with caves and Buddhist shrines, about 10 minutes south of My Khe.

  • Take the elevator up to save your legs, then walk down through the cave temples
  • Wear proper shoes; the stone steps get slippery and some passages are steep
  • Budget about two hours, and go early to beat both the heat and the tour buses

By early afternoon you're back at the coast with nothing on the schedule but rest. This is the day the trip is designed around: a long lunch, a nap, an unhurried swim, and maybe a spa treatment, which is excellent value in Da Nang. If you're a coffee person, a Vietnamese ca phe sua da (iced coffee with condensed milk) at a beachfront cafe is the correct way to spend an hour.

For a sunset with a view, the Son Tra Peninsula and the giant Lady Buddha statue at Linh Ung Pagoda are a short ride away and look out over the whole bay.

Day 4: Ba Na Hills or a Slow Departure

Your last day depends on your flight time and how much energy you have left. There are two honest options, and neither is wrong.

Option A: Ba Na Hills and the Golden Bridge

If your flight leaves in the evening, dedicate the morning to Ba Na Hills, the mountaintop resort about 45 minutes inland, home to the famous Golden Bridge held up by two giant stone hands. The cable car ride alone is worth it, and the temperature up top is noticeably cooler than the coast. It's a half-day at minimum once you factor in travel, so only choose this if you have a late departure and don't mind a busy morning.

Option B: A slow morning by the sea

If you'd rather leave feeling rested, keep day four simple: one more beach walk, a proper Vietnamese breakfast of banh mi or pho, and a bit of last-minute shopping for coffee beans and dried fruit to take home. Central Vietnam rewards a slow exit as much as a packed one.

Whichever you pick, give yourself a comfortable buffer to the airport. Da Nang's airport is close to the city, but check-in and security still eat time, and you don't want the last memory of the trip to be a sprint to the gate.

Where to Stay: Da Nang, Hoi An, or Both

The single biggest decision that shapes this trip is where you sleep. Here's how to think about it:

  • All nights in Da Nang (My Khe area): The simplest choice. You get resort pools, a long beach, easy airport access, and more dining variety, with Hoi An as a day trip. Best for first-timers and anyone who prioritizes beach time.
  • All nights in Hoi An: Quieter and more atmospheric, with the Old Town at your doorstep in the evenings. The trade-off is that the beach is a short ride away rather than out your window, and you'll commute for Da Nang activities.
  • Split: two nights Da Nang, one in Hoi An: The best of both if you don't mind repacking once. You get a real beach base plus an evening and morning in Hoi An without the after-dark drive back.

For most travelers doing this exact 4-day plan, two nights in Da Nang and one in Hoi An hits the sweet spot: you experience the lanterns at night and the Old Town before the crowds, then return to the coast for your relaxation day.

Practical Tips and a Realistic Budget

A few things worth knowing before you go:

  • Getting around: Ride-hailing apps work well in both cities and take the guesswork out of taxi fares. For Da Nang to Hoi An, a private car is comfortable; for short hops, motorbike taxis are cheap and quick.
  • Cash vs card: Vietnam is still cash-first for markets, street food, and small shops. Carry Vietnamese dong for daily spending and keep a card for hotels and bigger restaurants.
  • Bargaining: Expected at markets and with tailors, not at fixed-price cafes and restaurants. Keep it friendly.
  • Weather: Central Vietnam is hot and humid most of the year. Pack light, breathable clothes, sunscreen, and a compact rain layer for the shoulder months.

On budget, this trip is famously friendly to your wallet. Excluding flights, a comfortable mid-range traveler can expect roughly the following per day: a decent beachfront hotel from around 40 to 80 USD a night, meals for 15 to 30 USD across street food and sit-down restaurants, and 20 to 40 USD for transport and activities. Splurge on a resort or Ba Na Hills tickets and it climbs; lean into street food and it drops fast.

Central Vietnam gives you room to plan loosely and still have a great trip. Sketch the four days, leave space for a spontaneous tailor visit or an extra hour at the beach, and let the balance between rest and discovery do the rest.
